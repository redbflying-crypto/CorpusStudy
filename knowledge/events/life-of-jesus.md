# Summary

**Birth and youth.** Jesus was born in Bethlehem "in the days of Herod the king", and wise men came asking for "he that is born King of the Jews".[^kjv-matt2-1] He grew up in Nazareth and "increased in wisdom and stature, and in favour with God and man."[^kjv-luke2-52] His conception and birth are treated under [the incarnation](/doctrines/incarnation.md) and [Jesus Christ](/people/jesus.md).

**Preparation.** After his baptism by John the Baptist he was "led up of the Spirit into the wilderness to be tempted of the devil", and fasted "forty days and forty nights".[^kjv-matt4-1]

**Ministry in Galilee.** He "went about all Galilee, teaching in their synagogues, and preaching the gospel of the kingdom, and healing all manner of sickness".[^kjv-matt4-23] He chose twelve "that they should be with him, and that he might send them forth to preach".[^kjv-mark3-14] After the Sermon on the Mount the crowds were astonished, "For he taught them as one having authority, and not as the scribes."[^kjv-matt7-28]

**Turning toward the cross.** After Peter confessed, "Thou art the Christ", "he began to teach them, that the Son of man must suffer many things".[^kjv-mark8-31] On a mountain he "was transfigured before them: and his face did shine as the sun".[^kjv-matt17-1] Then "he stedfastly set his face to go to Jerusalem".[^kjv-luke9-51]

**The last week and after.** As he entered Jerusalem the crowds cried, "Hosanna to the Son of David".[^kjv-matt21-9] His arrest, death and rising are told under [the crucifixion](/events/crucifixion.md) and [the resurrection](/events/resurrection.md). He was then "seen of them forty days",[^kjv-acts1-3] and at Bethany, "while he blessed them, he was parted from them, and carried up into heaven."[^kjv-luke24-50]

# Context

The Gospels are four accounts of one life. Luke wrote "in order" after many others had drawn up accounts from "eyewitnesses, and ministers of the word".[^kjv-luke1-1] John says that "many other signs" are left out, and that "these are written, that ye might believe that Jesus is the Christ".[^kjv-john20-30] Matthew Henry notes that we may profit from "reviewing the same events, placed by each of the evangelists in that point of view which most affected his own mind".[^henry-mark-intro] A harmony of the Gospels arranges the four accounts into one sequence while keeping each writer's emphasis. Henry repeats the older view that Matthew "is generally allowed to have written his Gospel before any other of the evangelists".[^henry-matt-intro]

# Significance

In Reformed reading his whole life, not only his death, belongs to his saving work. His lowly birth came in "the fulness of time", and it rebukes our pride: seeing "the Son of God being made man and lying in a manger, our vanity, ambition, and envy are checked."[^henry-luke2] At his baptism he chose "to fulfil all righteousness, to own every Divine institution".[^henry-matt3] Reformed theology calls this his active obedience and holds that it is counted to believers: "by the obedience of one shall many be made righteous."[^kjv-rom5-19] His miracles "confirmed his Divine mission", and showed "that his great errand into the world was to cure spiritual maladies."[^henry-matt4-23]

**Where other traditions differ.** These are summaries for orientation, not drawn from the corpus:

- **Most modern scholars** hold that Mark was written first and used by Matthew and Luke. Many critical scholars doubt the infancy stories and the miracles; the Reformed tradition receives the four Gospels as true and inspired testimony.
- **Roman Catholic and Eastern Orthodox** traditions hold that Mary remained a virgin, and read the brothers of Jesus as cousins or as children of Joseph. Many Protestants take them as later children of Mary and Joseph.
