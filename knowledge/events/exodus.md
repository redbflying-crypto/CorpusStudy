# Summary

After a series of plagues on Egypt, God brought Israel out of slavery. On the night of the last plague each household killed a lamb and marked its doorposts with the blood: "when I see the blood, I will pass over you, and the plague shall not be upon you to destroy you".[^kjv-exod12-13] When Pharaoh's army pursued them to the sea, Moses told the people to "stand still, and see the salvation of the LORD".[^kjv-exod14-13] God opened a way through the waters and the pursuers were drowned: "Thus the LORD saved Israel that day out of the hand of the Egyptians", and the people "feared the LORD, and believed the LORD, and his servant Moses."[^kjv-exod14-30]

# Context

God had told Abram that his seed would be afflicted in a foreign land "four hundred years", and that "afterward shall they come out with great substance."[^kjv-gen15-13] Through [Moses](/people/moses.md) he announced his purpose: "I will redeem you with a stretched out arm" and "I will take you to me for a people".[^kjv-exod6-6] The plagues were a contest with Pharaoh and his religion: "against all the gods of Egypt I will execute judgment: I am the LORD."[^kjv-exod12-12] The story is told in the book of [Exodus](/books/exodus.md).

# Significance

The exodus is the defining act of redemption in the Old Testament. God grounds the Ten Commandments in it: "I am the LORD thy God, which have brought thee out of the land of Egypt, out of the house of bondage."[^kjv-exod20-2]

In Reformed reading, following the New Testament, the exodus also foreshadows Christ. Paul writes that "Christ our passover is sacrificed for us".[^kjv-1cor5-7] Matthew Henry calls the Passover "a remarkable type of Christ", and stresses that Israel's "safety and deliverance were not a reward of their own righteousness, but the gift of mercy."[^henry-exod12] See [the crucifixion](/events/crucifixion.md) and [atonement](/doctrines/atonement.md). Paul also says that Israel "were all baptized unto Moses in the cloud and in the sea";[^kjv-1cor10-1] Henry calls the crossing "a type of baptism" and the drowning of the Egyptians "typical of the final ruin of all unrepenting sinners."[^henry-exod14] See [sacraments](/doctrines/sacraments.md). Henry sees in the whole book a picture of "the church, in the wilderness of this world, until her arrival at the heavenly Canaan".[^henry-exod-intro]

**Where other traditions differ.** These are summaries for orientation, not drawn from the corpus:

- **Jewish** tradition keeps the exodus at the centre of its faith, remembered each year at Passover as the founding liberation of the people, without the Christian typology.
- **Liberation theologies** read the exodus mainly as a paradigm of God siding with the oppressed against political and economic bondage.
- **Historians** differ on the date of the exodus, and many critical scholars doubt that it happened on the scale described. The Reformed tradition receives it as a historical event.

Exodus 12:1-42 (the Passover and the departure); Exodus 14 (the crossing of the sea); Exodus 15:1-21 (the Song of Moses); 1 Corinthians 5:7; 1 Corinthians 10:1-4.
