# Summary

"When the day of Pentecost was fully come", the disciples were together in Jerusalem. A sound came "as of a rushing mighty wind", "cloven tongues like as of fire" rested on each of them, and "they were all filled with the Holy Ghost, and began to speak with other tongues, as the Spirit gave them utterance."[^kjv-acts2-1] Jews "out of every nation under heaven" heard them "speak in our tongues the wonderful works of God", though some mocked: "These men are full of new wine."[^kjv-acts2-5]

[Peter](/people/peter.md) answered that "this is that which was spoken by the prophet Joel": "I will pour out of my Spirit upon all flesh".[^kjv-acts2-16] Jesus, raised and "by the right hand of God exalted", had "received of the Father the promise of the Holy Ghost" and "shed forth this, which ye now see and hear."[^kjv-acts2-32] Peter called them to "Repent, and be baptized every one of you in the name of Jesus Christ for the remission of sins", promising "the gift of the Holy Ghost". About "three thousand souls" were added, and they "continued stedfastly in the apostles' doctrine and fellowship, and in breaking of bread, and in prayers."[^kjv-acts2-38]

# Context

Pentecost (Greek for fiftieth) was the feast of weeks. Israel counted "fifty days" from the offering of the first sheaf and then brought loaves as "the firstfruits unto the LORD".[^kjv-lev23-15] Matthew Henry, following Jewish tradition, says the feast remembered "the giving of the law, fifty days after the departure from Egypt", and "looked forward to the outpouring of the Holy Ghost, fifty days after Christ our Passover was sacrificed for us."[^henry-lev23]

God had promised through Joel, "I will pour out my spirit upon all flesh".[^kjv-joel2-28] Before his ascension Jesus told the disciples to "wait for the promise of the Father", for "ye shall be baptized with the Holy Ghost not many days hence."[^kjv-acts1-4] He added: "ye shall be witnesses unto me both in Jerusalem, and in all Judaea, and in Samaria, and unto the uttermost part of the earth."[^kjv-acts1-8]

# Significance

Henry writes that Joel's promise "began to be fulfilled on the day of Pentecost".[^henry-joel2] The outpouring was "the fruit of Christ's resurrection and ascension, and proof of both", and Peter's sermon shows that "the Spirit is given, not to do away the Scriptures, but to enable us to understand, approve, and obey them."[^henry-acts2-14] Pentecost began the church's mission to the nations. Calvin notes that the gathering of God's people "began conspicuously long ago at the time of Peter's first speech", when "great numbers from different countries joined in one confession of faith."[^calvin-isa43] See the book of [Acts](/books/acts.md).

Henry distinguishes the gift every believer receives from extraordinary gifts. The gift promised in Acts 2:38, "from which no true believer has ever been shut out, was that Spirit of adoption, that converting, guiding, sanctifying grace".[^henry-acts2-37] He calls spiritual gifts "extraordinary powers bestowed in the first ages, to convince unbelievers, and to spread the gospel."[^henry-1cor12] See [sanctification](/doctrines/sanctification.md) and [the Trinity](/doctrines/trinity.md).

**Where other traditions differ.** These are summaries for orientation, not drawn from the corpus:

- **Pentecostal** churches teach a baptism in the Spirit, often after conversion and usually marked by tongues. **Charismatic** Christians in many denominations expect all the gifts of 1 Corinthians 12 today (continuationism).
- **Cessationists**, including many Reformed churches, hold that the sign gifts ceased with the apostolic age and the completed Scripture, and that Pentecost, like the cross, is unrepeatable. Other Reformed Christians are open to the gifts but cautious, testing every claim by Scripture.
- **Roman Catholic and Eastern Orthodox** churches link the gift of the Spirit with confirmation or chrismation.
