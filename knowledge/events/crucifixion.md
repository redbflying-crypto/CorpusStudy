# Summary

After his arrest and trial, Jesus was handed over by Pontius Pilate, who "delivered Jesus, when he had scourged him, to be crucified."[^kjv-mark15-15] At the place called Calvary "they crucified him, and the malefactors, one on the right hand, and the other on the left", and Jesus prayed, "Father, forgive them; for they know not what they do."[^kjv-luke23-33] From the sixth hour to the ninth there was darkness, and Jesus cried, "My God, my God, why hast thou forsaken me?"[^kjv-mark15-33] At the end "he said, It is finished: and he bowed his head, and gave up the ghost."[^kjv-john19-30]

# Context

All four Gospels tell the story at length, and the apostles preached it as both God's plan and human crime: Jesus was "delivered by the determinate counsel and foreknowledge of God", and "by wicked hands" crucified.[^kjv-acts2-23] His death was foretold, above all in Psalm 22 and Isaiah 53 (see [Isaiah](/books/isaiah.md)). Calvin writes that "Since David was an image and foreshadowing of Christ, he was endowed with the Spirit of prophecy, and predicted the sufferings of Christ."[^calvin-matt27] It took place at Passover, the feast of [the Exodus](/events/exodus.md): "it was the preparation of the passover".[^kjv-john19-14]

Jesus went to his death willingly: "No man taketh it from me, but I lay it down of myself."[^kjv-john10-18] Matthew Henry notes that just before he died Christ spoke in his full strength, "to show that his life was not forced from him, but was freely delivered into his Father's hands."[^henry-matt27-45]

# Significance

In Reformed reading the cross is a substitutionary atonement: Christ bore the penalty of sin in the place of his people (see [atonement](/doctrines/atonement.md)). "Christ hath redeemed us from the curse of the law, being made a curse for us".[^kjv-gal3-13] He "his own self bare our sins in his own body on the tree".[^kjv-1pet2-24] Calvin puts it plainly: "Our sins overwhelm us; but they are transferred to Christ, in whom we are acquitted."[^calvin-isa53-6] Henry writes that in the darkness Jesus was "suffering his Father's displeasure against the sin of man".[^henry-matt27-45]

The work was complete. On "It is finished" Henry comments, "the work of man's redemption and salvation is now completed."[^henry-john19] The torn veil of the temple "signified that Christ, by his death, opened a way to God."[^henry-matt27-51] Calvin also reads the soldiers' stripping of Christ this way: "The Son was stripped by God's will, to clothe us with his righteousness".[^calvin-matt27] See [justification](/doctrines/justification.md) and [the resurrection](/events/resurrection.md).

**Where other traditions differ.** These are summaries for orientation, not drawn from the corpus:

- **Christus Victor** readings, strong in the early church and revived in modern theology, put the stress on Christ's victory over sin, death and the devil. Reformed theology affirms this victory but grounds it in penal substitution.
- **Moral influence** views, found in some medieval and many liberal Protestant writers, see the cross chiefly as a display of God's love that moves sinners to repent.
- The **governmental** view of some Arminians holds that the cross upholds God's moral government rather than paying the exact penalty owed.
- **Arminians, Lutherans and others** hold that Christ died for every person alike, where classic Reformed theology teaches particular redemption: Christ died to save the elect.
