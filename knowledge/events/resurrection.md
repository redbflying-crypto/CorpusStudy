# Summary

On the first day of the week, after the sabbath, women came to the tomb where Jesus had been buried. An angel told them: "He is not here: for he is risen, as he said. Come, see the place where the Lord lay."[^kjv-matt28-1] Jesus then showed himself alive in his own body: "handle me, and see; for a spirit hath not flesh and bones, as ye see me have."[^kjv-luke24-39] When Thomas was invited to touch his wounds he answered, "My Lord and my God."[^kjv-john20-27] Paul lists the witnesses: Cephas, the twelve, "above five hundred brethren at once", James, all the apostles, and last of all Paul himself.[^kjv-1cor15-3]

# Context

The resurrection follows [the crucifixion](/events/crucifixion.md) and was part of the earliest preaching: Christ "rose again the third day according to the scriptures".[^kjv-1cor15-3] Peter proclaimed that God raised him up, "because it was not possible that he should be holden of it."[^kjv-acts2-24] Matthew Henry notes that the third day "was the time he had often spoken of", and that the first day of the week became "the day which Christians religiously observed in solemn assemblies, to the honour of Christ."[^henry-matt28]

Most believers must trust the witnesses without seeing. Jesus' word to Thomas, "blessed are they who have not seen and have believed", leads Calvin to say that faith "does not in the least depend upon sense and carnal reason, but acquiesces in the mere Word of God."[^calvin-john20-29]

# Significance

In Reformed reading the resurrection is God's public verdict on Christ's person and work. Christ was "declared to be the Son of God with power ... by the resurrection from the dead".[^kjv-rom1-4] He "was delivered for our offences, and was raised again for our justification."[^kjv-rom4-25] Henry explains: "By his death he paid our debt, in his resurrection he received our acquittance".[^henry-rom4] See [justification](/doctrines/justification.md).

Everything rests on it: "if Christ be not raised, your faith is vain; ye are yet in your sins."[^kjv-1cor15-17] Henry calls "The doctrine of Christ's death and resurrection" "the foundation of Christianity."[^henry-1cor15-1] Christ is "the firstfruits of them that slept",[^kjv-1cor15-20] and Henry concludes that "All that are by faith united to Christ, are by his resurrection assured of their own."[^henry-1cor15-20] The risen life also shapes the present, so that believers "should walk in newness of life."[^kjv-rom6-4] See [sanctification](/doctrines/sanctification.md) and [Paul](/people/paul.md).

**Where other traditions differ.** These are summaries for orientation, not drawn from the corpus:

- **Roman Catholic, Eastern Orthodox and Protestant** churches alike confess the bodily resurrection in the historic creeds; the Orthodox make Pascha the centre of the whole church year.
- Some **modern liberal** theologians interpret the resurrection as a spiritual reality or as the rise of the disciples' faith, without a bodily rising or empty tomb. Reformed teaching, with historic Christianity, holds that the same body that died was raised.
