# Definition

Election is God's eternal choice of particular persons for salvation in Christ. God "hath chosen us in him before the foundation of the world", "Having predestinated us unto the adoption of children by Jesus Christ to himself, according to the good pleasure of his will".[^kjv-eph1-4] Predestination names the wider purpose "of him who worketh all things after the counsel of his own will".[^kjv-eph1-11] Paul sets election at the head of a sequence: "whom he did predestinate, them he also called: and whom he called, them he also justified: and whom he justified, them he also glorified."[^kjv-rom8-29]

# Reformed view

Reformed teaching holds that election is unconditional: it rests on God's free mercy, not on faith or merit foreseen in the person. Jacob was chosen before birth, "neither having done any good or evil, that the purpose of God according to election might stand, not of works, but of him that calleth"; "it is not of him that willeth, nor of him that runneth, but of God that sheweth mercy."[^kjv-rom9-11] Calvin calls it "a childish cavil devised by sophistry" to say God chose those he "foresaw that we would be worthy", because "we are all lost in Adam."[^calvin-eph1] God's foreknowledge in Romans 8:29 "is not a mere knowing beforehand", he argues, but "the act of adoption".[^calvin-rom8]

On this view election is the cause of faith, not its reward. "All that the Father giveth me shall come to me; and him that cometh to me I will in no wise cast out."[^kjv-john6-37] And "No man can come to me, except the Father which hath sent me draw him".[^kjv-john6-44] Henry comments that "No one will come, till Divine grace has subdued, and in part changed his heart; therefore no one who comes will ever be cast out."[^henry-john6] He calls Romans 8:29-30 "a golden chain, one which cannot be broken".[^henry-rom8]

Election does not license careless living. Henry writes: "All who are chosen to happiness as the end, are chosen to holiness as the means."[^henry-eph1] Calvin says "it is vicious to separate the holiness of life from the grace of election".[^calvin-eph1] Nor is it a puzzle to be solved by speculation. Calvin warns against seeking salvation "in the labyrinth of predestination"; the believer's calling "should be to us sufficient evidence of our salvation as though it were his seal cut into us."[^calvin-john6]

Reformed teaching also speaks of reprobation, God passing by others. Henry holds the two sides together: "those who are saved, must thank God only; and those who perish, must blame themselves only".[^henry-rom9] Calvin locates the cause of the ruin of the lost "in the perversity of natures which God has left alone", yet admits that "the cause of eternal rejection is so hidden that there is nothing left for us to do but to be amazed at the incomprehensible mind of God".[^calvin-rom11]

# Where other traditions differ

These are summaries for orientation, not drawn from the corpus:

- **Arminian and Wesleyan** traditions teach conditional election: God elects those he foreknows will believe and persevere, and by prevenient grace enables every hearer to respond. Some Arminians stress the corporate election of the church in Christ.
- **Lutheran** teaching (Formula of Concord, Article XI) affirms that election to salvation is wholly of grace and a cause of salvation, but denies any predestination to damnation: the lost perish by their own fault. It declines to resolve the tension rationally.
- **Roman Catholic** teaching affirms predestination to glory as God's gracious work and rejects any predestination to evil (Council of Orange, 529; Council of Trent). Thomists and Molinists differ on how grace and free will relate, and the Church has left that debate open.
- **Amyraldian** theology (Moïse Amyraut, Saumur) is a minority view within the Reformed family. It orders God's decrees so that a conditional decree to save all who believe precedes the unconditional election of some (see [atonement](/doctrines/atonement.md)).
