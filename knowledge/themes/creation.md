# Overview

Scripture opens with God as Creator: "In the beginning God created the heaven and the earth."[^kjv-gen1-1] Creation teaches that God alone is eternal and self-sufficient, that everything else depends on him, and that the world as he made it was good: "God saw every thing that he had made, and, behold, it was very good."[^kjv-gen1-31]

# Development through Scripture

God created by speaking: "By the word of the LORD were the heavens made".[^kjv-ps33-6] Humanity is the summit of the work: "God created man in his own image".[^kjv-gen1-27] Creation also reveals its Maker to all people, so that his "eternal power and Godhead" are "clearly seen, being understood by the things that are made", leaving them "without excuse".[^kjv-rom1-20]

The New Testament names the Son as the agent of creation: "All things were made by him".[^kjv-john1-3] "All things were created by him, and for him", and "by him all things consist".[^kjv-col1-16] Since the [fall](/events/fall.md), "the creature was made subject to vanity", but in hope that it "shall be delivered from the bondage of corruption".[^kjv-rom8-20] Scripture ends with "a new heaven and a new earth".[^kjv-rev21-1]

# Reformed view

Reformed theology holds that God made the world out of nothing. Hebrews says "things which are seen were not made of things which do appear".[^kjv-heb11-3] Calvin argues from the verb in Genesis 1:1 that "the world was created from nothing", and he rejects the view "that formless matter was always in existence".[^calvin-gen1-1]

Creation is Trinitarian. Matthew Henry notes that "the Son of God, one with the Father, was with him when he made the world", and that the Spirit appears "at the beginning of the sacred volume".[^henry-gen1-1] Calvin, however, cautions against using the plural Elohim of Genesis 1:1 as proof of the Trinity, saying "this does not seem a solid proof for so great a truth".[^calvin-gen1-1]

The image of God sets humanity apart. Henry writes that "it is the soul of man that especially bears God's image", that "man was made upright", and that the image is now "defaced" and must be renewed by grace.[^henry-gen1-26] Creation also groans under sin. Henry speaks of "an impurity, deformity, and infirmity, which has come upon the creature by the fall of man", yet "God will deliver it".[^henry-rom8]

Calvin holds that Genesis speaks in ordinary language: Moses "described in popular style what all ordinary men without training and education perceive with their ordinary senses". He therefore saw no conflict with astronomy, which "admirably reveals the wisdom of God".[^calvin-gen1-16] Reformed interpreters today differ over the length of the days of Genesis 1 and the age of the earth; this summary takes no position on that question.
