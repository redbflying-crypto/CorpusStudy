# Overview

Justice in Scripture is first a truth about God: he always does what is right. Abraham asked, "Shall not the Judge of all the earth do right?"[^kjv-gen18-25] Moses sang that "all his ways are judgment: a God of truth and without iniquity, just and right is he."[^kjv-deut32-4] The KJV often calls it judgment or righteousness. This theme concerns God's character and the right dealing he requires of people. It is distinct from [justification](/doctrines/justification.md), God's verdict declaring a believing sinner righteous, though the two meet at the cross.

# Development through Scripture

"Justice and judgment are the habitation of thy throne".[^kjv-ps89-14] God "regardeth not persons, nor taketh reward", and he "doth execute the judgment of the fatherless and widow, and loveth the stranger".[^kjv-deut10-17] So his people must "seek judgment, relieve the oppressed, judge the fatherless, plead for the widow."[^kjv-isa1-17]

The prophets condemn worship that ignores injustice. Through Amos God rejects feasts and offerings and demands instead: "let judgment run down as waters, and righteousness as a mighty stream."[^kjv-amos5] Micah asks what the LORD requires, "but to do justly, and to love mercy, and to walk humbly with thy God?"[^kjv-mic6-8]

At the cross God's justice and mercy meet. God set forth Christ "to be a propitiation through faith in his blood, to declare his righteousness", so "that he might be just, and the justifier of him which believeth in Jesus."[^kjv-rom3-25] Believers are not to take revenge: "Vengeance is mine; I will repay, saith the Lord."[^kjv-rom12-19] God "hath appointed a day, in the which he will judge the world in righteousness" by the risen Christ.[^kjv-acts17-31]

# Reformed view

In Reformed reading God's justice is not set aside when he saves; it is satisfied (see [atonement](/doctrines/atonement.md)). Henry on Romans 3: "It is plain that he hates sin, when nothing less than the blood of Christ would satisfy for it. And it would not agree with his justice to demand the debt, when the Surety has paid it".[^henry-rom3] For sinners, God's justice alone would bring only dread. Calvin asks, "what use would it be to us to know that God is just unless we already know his mercy and his free kindness?"[^calvin-jer9]

Doing justice is the fruit of faith, not the price of pardon. Henry: "The good which God requires of us is, not the paying a price for the pardon of sin and acceptance with God, but love to himself".[^henry-mic6] Calvin takes the first two demands of Micah 6:8 to refer to the second table of the law. He calls "justice which is practiced among men" the "true evidence of devotion to God".[^calvin-mic6] For Calvin, Christ the King does justice "by defending the harmless and aiding the oppressed, by helping the poor and checking the wicked", and "also he establishes justice in regenerating us by his Spirit."[^calvin-jer23]

**Where other traditions differ.** These are summaries for orientation, not drawn from the corpus. **Roman Catholic** social teaching has developed an extensive doctrine of justice and the common good. **Liberation theology**, especially in Latin America, reads the Exodus and the prophets as centring God's justice on freeing the oppressed; critics, including many Reformed writers, say it can reduce salvation to social change. Some modern Protestant scholars read God's righteousness in Romans mainly as covenant faithfulness rather than as retributive justice satisfied at the cross.
