# Overview

Holiness is first a quality of God himself: his majesty set apart from every creature and his perfect moral purity. Israel sang, "who is like thee, glorious in holiness, fearful in praises, doing wonders?"[^kjv-exod15-11] Derived from this, holiness describes people, places and things set apart for God, and the purity of life he requires: "Ye shall be holy: for I the LORD your God am holy."[^kjv-lev19-2]

# Development through Scripture

At the burning bush Moses was told, "put off thy shoes from off thy feet, for the place whereon thou standest is holy ground."[^kjv-exod3-5] Matthew Henry notes that fire in Scripture is "an emblem ... of the Divine holiness and justice", and that "the more we see of God, and his grace, and covenant love, the more cause we shall see to worship him with reverence and godly fear."[^henry-exod3] The law of Moses tied holiness to the distinction of clean and unclean: "ye shall be holy; for I am holy: neither shall ye defile yourselves".[^kjv-lev11-44]

Isaiah heard the seraphim cry "Holy, holy, holy, is the LORD of hosts", and responded, "Woe is me! for I am undone; because I am a man of unclean lips".[^kjv-isa6-3] The New Testament carries the command over to the church: "as he which hath called you is holy, so be ye holy in all manner of conversation".[^kjv-1pet1-15] God disciplines his children "that we might be partakers of his holiness",[^kjv-heb12-10] and believers must follow "holiness, without which no man shall see the Lord".[^kjv-heb12-14]

# Reformed view

Reformed teaching begins with God's holiness, which exposes human sin. On Isaiah 6 Calvin writes that "before our minds seriously approach God, our life is an empty sham", and that "when God comes to us, he brings light with him, and we see our emptiness".[^calvin-isa6] Henry draws the same conclusion: "We are undone if there is not a Mediator between us and this holy God."[^henry-isa6]

Holiness in believers flows from grace, not the reverse. Calvin reads Ephesians 1:4 as teaching that "sanctity, innocence, and every virtue among men, is the fruit of election", and that it is "vicious to separate the holiness of life from the grace of election".[^calvin-eph1] Henry puts it briefly: "God makes those holy whom he saves."[^henry-1pet1] Because believers belong to God, Calvin says on Romans 12:1, "our whole life should be an exercise in holiness".[^calvin-rom12] This growth is the work of [sanctification](/doctrines/sanctification.md), which Reformed theology distinguishes from, but never separates from, [justification](/doctrines/justification.md). It remains incomplete in this life; Calvin calls perfection "the goal toward which we strive throughout the course of our lives".[^calvin-eph1]

**Where other traditions differ.** These are summaries for orientation, not drawn from the corpus. **Wesleyan and Holiness** traditions teach that entire sanctification or Christian perfection, understood as perfect love, can be received in this life as a second work of grace. **Roman Catholic** teaching places the growth of holiness within the sacramental life of the church and holds up canonised saints as models. **Keswick** teaching speaks of a deeper life entered by surrender and faith.
