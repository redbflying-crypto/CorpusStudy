# Overview

The kingdom of God is God's rule as King. In one sense it has always been universal: "his kingdom ruleth over all."[^kjv-ps103-19] In the sense the Gospels announce, it is the saving reign God promised to establish through his Messiah. To [David](/people/david.md) God promised a son whose throne he would "stablish ... for ever".[^kjv-2sam7-12] Jesus proclaimed its arrival: "The time is fulfilled, and the kingdom of God is at hand: repent ye, and believe the gospel."[^kjv-mark1-15]

# Development through Scripture

The prophets foresaw a kingdom unlike the empires of the world. Daniel saw that "the God of heaven set up a kingdom, which shall never be destroyed".[^kjv-dan2-44] One "like the Son of man" receives "dominion, and glory, and a kingdom, that all people, nations, and languages, should serve him".[^kjv-dan7-14]

Jesus showed that the kingdom had arrived in his own ministry: "if I cast out devils by the Spirit of God, then the kingdom of God is come unto you."[^kjv-matt12-28] Yet he told Pilate, "My kingdom is not of this world".[^kjv-john18-36] Believers have already been "translated ... into the kingdom of his dear Son",[^kjv-col1-13] and still pray "Thy kingdom come".[^kjv-matt6-10] Christ "must reign, till he hath put all enemies under his feet", and at the end he will deliver up "the kingdom to God, even the Father".[^kjv-1cor15-24]

# Reformed view

Calvin distinguishes two ways in which Scripture speaks of God's rule. God governs the devil and all the wicked, who "obey God against their wish and will". But "God's special rule applies to his church alone, where God by his Word and Spirit turns the hearts of men to obedience". This, he says, is "the Kingdom of God".[^calvin-mic4] The kingdom therefore advances chiefly through preaching, not force. Matthew Henry says Christ's kingdom is "set up in their hearts and consciences", and that "its weapons are spiritual".[^henry-john18]

The kingdom is already present but not yet complete. Calvin writes that "the reign of Christ had already begun" with the preaching of the gospel, "and today it is still in progress and not yet complete".[^calvin-mic4] Until then the church faces opposition, for "there shall never be quietness for the Kingdom of Christ in this world"; but its enemies "shall not prevail, because Christ sits at God's right hand".[^calvin-heb1-13] Calvin also insists the kingdom is not limited to one nation, since "the Kingship of Christ extends to the ends of the world".[^calvin-john1-49] Henry reads Daniel's stone as Christ's kingdom, destined to fill "the whole earth".[^henry-dan2]

**Where other traditions differ.** These are summaries for orientation, not drawn from the corpus. Christians differ chiefly over the millennium of Revelation 20. **Amillennialism** (widely held in Reformed churches) takes it as the present reign of Christ between his two comings. **Postmillennialism** (also held by many Reformed writers) expects the gospel to bring a long era of widespread faith before Christ returns. **Historic premillennialism** expects an earthly reign of Christ after his return. **Dispensational premillennialism** further distinguishes the church from national Israel and expects a future earthly kingdom centred on Israel.
