# Overview

Worship is the creature's response to God's worth: "O come, let us worship and bow down: let us kneel before the LORD our maker."[^kjv-ps95-6] It belongs to God alone, and it must be offered as he desires: "they that worship him must worship him in spirit and in truth."[^kjv-john4-23]

# Development through Scripture

The second commandment forbids worship through images: "Thou shalt not make unto thee any graven image ... Thou shalt not bow down thyself to them".[^kjv-exod20-4] When Nadab and Abihu "offered strange fire before the LORD, which he commanded them not", fire "devoured them", and God declared, "I will be sanctified in them that come nigh me".[^kjv-lev10-1] Israel was told, "thou shalt not add thereto, nor diminish from it."[^kjv-deut12-32]

Jesus rebuked worship shaped by human tradition: "in vain they do worship me, teaching for doctrines the commandments of men."[^kjv-matt15-9] He announced that "the true worshippers shall worship the Father in spirit and in truth".[^kjv-john4-23] Paul calls believers to present their bodies "a living sacrifice, holy, acceptable unto God, which is your reasonable service",[^kjv-rom12-1] and Hebrews urges them to "serve God acceptably with reverence and godly fear".[^kjv-heb12-28]

# Reformed view

Reformed churches hold the regulative principle: in public worship the church should do only what God has commanded or authorized in Scripture. Matthew Henry reads the second commandment as forbidding "all kinds of superstition" and "the using of mere human inventions in the worship of God".[^henry-exod20] On Matthew 15 he warns, "Never let us think that the religion of the Bible can be improved by any human addition, either in doctrine or practice."[^henry-matt15]

Worship is spiritual. Calvin explains that it is called spiritual "because nothing can take the place of the inward faith of the heart". In every age God "desired to be worshiped by faith, prayer, acts of thanksgiving, purity of heart, and innocence of life". Under the law this was hidden under ceremonies, but now "what was shadowy to the fathers, we now have openly and clearly".[^calvin-john4] Acceptable worship rests on Christ's sacrifice: "all worship is unclean and wicked unless purified by the sprinkling of the blood of Christ."[^calvin-heb9]

Worship also extends to the whole of life. On Romans 12:1 Calvin says believers have "by his grace ... been made priests, that we may dedicate all we have to the glory of God".[^calvin-rom12] Praise joins the church to heaven: "the holiest service of all that we can offer God is to occupy ourselves in praising his name".[^calvin-isa6]

**Where other traditions differ.** These are summaries for orientation, not drawn from the corpus. **Lutheran and Anglican** churches generally follow the normative principle: practices not forbidden by Scripture may be used if they edify. **Roman Catholic and Eastern Orthodox** worship centres on the eucharistic liturgy and gives a place to images or icons, which they distinguish from idolatry. **Reformed** churches themselves differ in applying the regulative principle, for example on exclusive psalmody, musical instruments and the church calendar.
