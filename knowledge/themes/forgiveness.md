# Overview

Forgiveness is God's release of sinners from the guilt and penalty of their sin, and the matching release that forgiven people grant to those who wrong them. "Blessed is he whose transgression is forgiven, whose sin is covered."[^kjv-ps32] Matthew Henry writes: "It is our unspeakable comfort that there is forgiveness with him, for that is what we need."[^henry-ps130]

# Development through Scripture

At Sinai God proclaimed himself as "forgiving iniquity and transgression and sin", and yet as one "that will by no means clear the guilty".[^kjv-exod34-7] David confessed and was pardoned: "I acknowledged my sin unto thee ... and thou forgavest the iniquity of my sin."[^kjv-ps32] Micah marvels at a God "that pardoneth iniquity", who will "cast all their sins into the depths of the sea."[^kjv-mic7]

The New Testament grounds forgiveness in Christ's death: "In whom we have redemption through his blood, the forgiveness of sins".[^kjv-eph1-7] On the cross Jesus prayed, "Father, forgive them; for they know not what they do."[^kjv-luke23-34] For believers who still sin, "If we confess our sins, he is faithful and just to forgive us our sins".[^kjv-1john1-9]

Forgiven people must forgive. Jesus taught his disciples to pray, "forgive us our debts, as we forgive our debtors", and warned, "if ye forgive not men their trespasses, neither will your Father forgive your trespasses."[^kjv-matt6] When Peter asked whether seven times was enough, Jesus answered "Until seventy times seven", and told of a servant forgiven ten thousand talents who would not forgive a fellow servant a hundred pence.[^kjv-matt18] Paul writes: "forgiving one another, even as God for Christ's sake hath forgiven you."[^kjv-eph4-32]

# Reformed view

In Reformed reading, God forgives without setting aside his justice, because Christ bore the penalty (see [atonement](/doctrines/atonement.md)). Henry says the believer's transgressions "are all forgiven, being covered with the atonement", and "Not to impute sin, is God's act, for he is the Judge."[^henry-ps32] Forgiveness is thus one side of [justification](/doctrines/justification.md). It is complete: "when God forgives sin, he forgives all", and God "will break the power of sin" as well as take away its guilt.[^henry-mic7]

Calvin holds that the gospel's chief aim is that "men be reconciled to God by the free remission of sins". Ministers declare that pardon, but Christ "alone remits sins".[^calvin-john20]

Our forgiving others is not the ground of God's pardon but its fruit. Henry: "the comfortable sense of pardoning mercy, does much to dispose our hearts to forgive our brethren." The parable's harsh ending does not mean God forgives and then revokes. It exposes those who "never entered into the spirit, or experienced the sanctifying grace of the gospel". Forgiveness must come "from the heart". It does not "make light of wronging our neighbour", but it refuses to "study revenge".[^henry-matt18] Henry sums it up: "Mark how God's forgiveness causes us to forgive."[^henry-eph4]

**Where other traditions differ.** These are summaries for orientation, not drawn from the corpus. **Roman Catholic** teaching holds that serious sins after baptism are ordinarily forgiven through the sacrament of penance (confession to a priest and absolution), and that temporal punishment may remain after guilt is forgiven. **Eastern Orthodox** churches also practise sacramental confession, often describing forgiveness as healing. Reformed churches teach confession to God directly, with the church declaring pardon through the preached word.
