# Overview

"God is love", and "Herein is love, not that we loved God, but that he loved us, and sent his Son to be the propitiation for our sins."[^kjv-1john4] Scripture speaks both of God's love for sinners and of the love he commands: love for God and for one's neighbour, on which "hang all the law and the prophets."[^kjv-matt22]

# Development through Scripture

The law commands Israel: "thou shalt love the LORD thy God with all thine heart, and with all thy soul, and with all thy might."[^kjv-deut6-5] Leviticus adds: "thou shalt love thy neighbour as thyself: I am the LORD."[^kjv-lev19-18] God's love for Israel did not rest on anything in them: "The LORD did not set his love upon you, nor choose you, because ye were more in number ... But because the LORD loved you".[^kjv-deut7-7]

In the gospel that love is shown in the gift of the Son: "For God so loved the world, that he gave his only begotten Son".[^kjv-john3-16] "God commendeth his love toward us, in that, while we were yet sinners, Christ died for us."[^kjv-rom5-8] Jesus joins the two great commandments, love of God first and love of neighbour "like unto it".[^kjv-matt22] He gives "A new commandment ... That ye love one another; as I have loved you".[^kjv-john13-34] That love reaches even enemies: "Love your enemies, bless them that curse you".[^kjv-matt5-44]

In 1 Corinthians 13 the KJV renders love as "charity": without it gifts and sacrifice profit nothing; "Charity suffereth long, and is kind"; and "the greatest of these is charity."[^kjv-1cor13] Henry explains that charity here is not almsgiving "but love in its fullest meaning; true love to God and man."[^henry-1cor13a]

# Reformed view

In Reformed reading God's love is free, not a response to human worth, and it is the source of salvation (see [grace](/themes/grace.md) and [election](/doctrines/election.md)). Calvin says John 3:16 is meant to "establish the love of God as the ground of our salvation", for Scripture praises "his pure and unmixed mercy, which does away with all merit". He reads the verse as a call that "invites all men without exception to faith in Christ", while "God opens the eyes of the elect alone".[^calvin-john3]

Human love answers God's: "We love him, because he first loved us."[^kjv-1john4-19] Calvin says love is called the perfection of the law "not because it is better than the worship of God, but because it is the convincing evidence of it", and "It is certainly wrong to separate the love of God from the love of man."[^calvin-gal5] For Calvin the neighbour is "the whole of the human species", and true love "has no regard for anybody's merit".[^calvin-matt5] Henry looks ahead to heaven: "There we shall perfectly love God. And there we shall perfectly love one another."[^henry-1cor13c]

**Where other traditions differ.** These are summaries for orientation, not drawn from the corpus. **Roman Catholic** teaching treats charity as a theological virtue infused by grace, and holds that faith justifies when it is formed by charity. Calvin attacked those who treated the love of enemies as a counsel of perfection rather than a command.[^calvin-matt5] **Arminian and Wesleyan** theology holds that God loves all people with the same saving intent, and Wesley taught that believers may be made perfect in love in this life. **Eastern Orthodox** theology speaks of love as sharing in the life of the Trinity.
