# Overview

Salvation is God's rescue of sinners from sin, its guilt and its penalty, to share eternal life with him. Jonah confessed: "Salvation is of the LORD."[^kjv-jonah2-9] It comes by grace through faith: "by grace are ye saved through faith; and that not of yourselves: it is the gift of God: Not of works, lest any man should boast."[^kjv-eph2-8]

# Development through Scripture

Facing the Egyptian army, Moses told Israel to "stand still, and see the salvation of the LORD".[^kjv-exod14-13] The angel told Joseph the meaning of Jesus' name: "he shall save his people from their sins."[^kjv-matt1-21] Peter preached that "there is none other name under heaven given among men, whereby we must be saved."[^kjv-acts4-12]

Paul describes the whole work: "Not by works of righteousness which we have done, but according to his mercy he saved us, by the washing of regeneration, and renewing of the Holy Ghost".[^kjv-titus3] Salvation has a present working out, "work out your own salvation with fear and trembling. For it is God which worketh in you".[^kjv-phil2-12] It also has a future completion, as believers "are kept by the power of God through faith unto salvation ready to be revealed in the last time."[^kjv-1pet1]

# Reformed view

Reformed theology reads Romans 8:29-30 as an unbroken sequence: "whom he did predestinate, them he also called: and whom he called, them he also justified: and whom he justified, them he also glorified."[^kjv-rom8-29] Henry calls it "the order of the causes of our salvation, a golden chain, one which cannot be broken", and he describes the call as "an effectual call".[^henry-rom8] Calvin holds that "all things which issue in the salvation of the saints depend upon the free election of God as their first cause". He himself reads predestinate in this verse narrowly, of God's decree that his children bear the cross and be conformed to Christ.[^calvin-rom8]

Reformed teaching names these aspects of the one salvation:

- **Election**, God's free choice before time (see [election](/doctrines/election.md)).
- **Calling**, God's summons given "not only through external preaching, but also through the accompanying power of the Spirit".[^calvin-rom8]
- **Justification**, the verdict of righteousness received by faith (see [justification](/doctrines/justification.md)).
- **Adoption**, by which God's people "receive the adoption of sons" and cry "Abba, Father".[^kjv-gal4] Henry: "by nature children of wrath and disobedience, they become by grace children of love".[^henry-gal4]
- **Sanctification**, the Spirit's renewing work (see [sanctification](/doctrines/sanctification.md)). Henry: "Works must be in the saved, but are not among the causes of their salvation."[^henry-titus3]
- **Glorification**: "we shall be like him; for we shall see him as he is."[^kjv-1john3-2]

Because salvation rests in God's hands, it is secure: Calvin writes that "the salvation of all the elect is as certain as that God's power is invincible."[^calvin-john10] See [perseverance](/doctrines/perseverance.md).

**Where other traditions differ.** These are summaries for orientation, not drawn from the corpus. **Arminian and Wesleyan** theology teaches that election rests on God's foreknowledge of faith, that prevenient grace enables all to believe but can be resisted, and (in many forms) that a believer can finally fall away. **Roman Catholic** teaching sees salvation as received through grace in the Church and its sacraments, beginning in baptism; justification includes inner renewal, can be lost by mortal sin, and is restored through penance. **Eastern Orthodox** theology describes salvation as healing and union with God (theosis) within the Church's sacramental life, and does not set out a fixed order of salvation.
