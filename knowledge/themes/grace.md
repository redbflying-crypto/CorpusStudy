# Overview

Grace is God's favour shown to those who do not deserve it. Paul sets it against every form of earning: "if by grace, then is it no more of works: otherwise grace is no more grace."[^kjv-rom11-6] Matthew Henry defines it as "the free, undeserved goodness and favour of God".[^henry-eph2]

# Development through Scripture

The word appears early: "Noah found grace in the eyes of the LORD."[^kjv-gen6-8] At Sinai God proclaimed his own name as "merciful and gracious, longsuffering, and abundant in goodness and truth".[^kjv-exod34-6] John contrasts the two great mediators of revelation: "the law was given by Moses, but grace and truth came by Jesus Christ", and from Christ's fulness "have all we received, and grace for grace".[^kjv-john1-16]

Paul makes grace the source of justification: believers are "justified freely by his grace through the redemption that is in Christ Jesus".[^kjv-rom3-24] Salvation as a whole is "by grace ... through faith; and that not of yourselves: it is the gift of God: Not of works, lest any man should boast."[^kjv-eph2-8] Grace also trains believers, "teaching us that, denying ungodliness and worldly lusts, we should live soberly, righteously, and godly".[^kjv-titus2-11]

# Reformed view

Reformed theology stresses that grace is sovereign and effectual: it does not merely assist a sinner who makes the first move, but gives life to one who is spiritually dead. Henry writes that faith, conversion and salvation "are not of works", and that "all is the free gift of God, and the effect of being quickened by his power".[^henry-eph2] Grace and merit cannot be combined: "Salvation from the first to the last must be either of grace or of debt."[^henry-rom11]

Grace is rooted in [election](/doctrines/election.md). Calvin calls it a "childish cavil" to say God chose people because he foresaw they would be worthy, since "we are all lost in Adam".[^calvin-eph1] He adds that the saints before Christ were saved by the same grace: "Abel, Noah, Abraham, Moses, David, and all the godly, obtained the same salvation with us".[^calvin-2tim1] For Calvin, grace "in general" means "the free forgiveness of sins and the renewal of the heart", so it includes both [justification](/doctrines/justification.md) and sanctification.[^calvin-john1]

Reformed writers also speak of God's goodness to all people, often called common grace. Calvin sees in the skills of Cain's descendants "the riches of his grace which God pours out on them", yet "we marvel much more at the grace of regeneration given us".[^calvin-gen4]

**Where other traditions differ.** These are summaries for orientation, not drawn from the corpus. **Pelagianism**, condemned by the early church, held that human nature can obey God without inward grace. **Semi-Pelagian** views let the sinner take the first step toward God. **Arminian and Wesleyan** theology holds that prevenient grace, given to all, restores the ability to believe, but that it can be resisted. **Roman Catholic** teaching affirms the necessity of grace but understands it as infused into the soul, with grace-enabled works being truly meritorious.
