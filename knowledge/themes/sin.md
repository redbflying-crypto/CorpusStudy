# Overview

Sin is rebellion against God and his law: "sin is the transgression of the law."[^kjv-1john3-4] It is first of all an offence against God himself, as David confessed: "Against thee, thee only, have I sinned".[^kjv-ps51-4] Its effect is separation and death: "your iniquities have separated between you and your God",[^kjv-isa59-2] and "the wages of sin is death".[^kjv-rom6-23]

# Development through Scripture

God warned [Adam](/people/adam.md) that "in the day that thou eatest thereof thou shalt surely die."[^kjv-gen2-17] Matthew Henry sums up Adam's act at the [fall](/events/fall.md) as "disobedience to a plain, easy, and express command"; Adam "had no corrupt nature within, to betray him", yet "he drew all his posterity into sin and ruin."[^henry-gen3] By Noah's day "every imagination of the thoughts of his heart was only evil continually."[^kjv-gen6-5] David traced his sin to its root: "Behold, I was shapen in iniquity; and in sin did my mother conceive me."[^kjv-ps51-4]

Paul draws the conclusion for all humanity: "all have sinned, and come short of the glory of God".[^kjv-rom3-23] Sin and death came "by one man", and "so death passed upon all men, for that all have sinned".[^kjv-rom5-12]

# Reformed view

Reformed theology teaches original sin: Adam's first sin brought both guilt and corruption on his descendants. Henry writes that "Adam sinning, his nature became guilty and corrupted, and so came to his children", and that "the sin of Adam extended to all his posterity".[^henry-rom5] Henry adds that the penitent "will ever trace back the streams of actual sin to the fountain of original depravity".[^henry-ps51]

Total depravity means that corruption reaches every part of human nature, not that people are as wicked as they could be. Calvin reasons from the new birth: it "means not the improvement of a part but the renewal of the whole of one's nature. It follows that there is nothing in us that is not corrupted."[^calvin-john3] The will is in bondage. On Romans 8:7 Calvin says "it is impossible for us to bring our powers into subjection so as to obey the law", and urges each person to know himself as the "servant of sin".[^calvin-rom8] Yet fallen people keep real gifts: Calvin reads John as denying "that the light of intelligence is entirely extinct", while holding that "natural reason can never guide men to Christ"; salvation is "hopeless unless God come to their aid with a new help" (see [grace](/themes/grace.md)).[^calvin-john1-5]

**Where other traditions differ.** These are summaries for orientation, not drawn from the corpus. **Pelagians** denied that Adam's sin corrupted his descendants. **Roman Catholic** teaching affirms original sin but holds that baptism removes its guilt, leaving concupiscence, which is not itself sin; the will is wounded but not wholly unable. **Eastern Orthodox** theology stresses inherited mortality and corruption rather than inherited guilt. **Arminian and Wesleyan** theology affirms depravity but holds that prevenient grace restores the ability to respond to God.
