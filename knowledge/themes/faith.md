# Overview

Faith is trust in God and in what he has promised, above all his promise of salvation in Jesus Christ. Hebrews describes it as "the substance of things hoped for, the evidence of things not seen", and adds that "without faith it is impossible to please him".[^kjv-heb11] Matthew Henry calls it "a firm persuasion and expectation, that God will perform all he has promised to us in Christ".[^henry-heb11]

# Development through Scripture

Abraham "believed in the LORD; and he counted it to him for righteousness."[^kjv-gen15-6] Through the prophet Habakkuk God declared that "the just shall live by his faith".[^kjv-hab2-4] Hebrews 11 follows faith through Abel, Enoch, Noah, Abraham, Moses, Rahab and many others, each commended for acting "by faith".[^kjv-heb11]

In the New Testament faith rests on Christ himself: "Believe on the Lord Jesus Christ, and thou shalt be saved".[^kjv-acts16-31] It comes through the preached word: "faith cometh by hearing, and hearing by the word of God."[^kjv-rom10-17] Paul takes up Habakkuk's words: "The just shall live by faith."[^kjv-rom1-17] Weak faith is still faith: "Lord, I believe; help thou mine unbelief."[^kjv-mark9-24]

James warns against a faith of words only: "faith, if it hath not works, is dead, being alone", and "the devils also believe, and tremble."[^kjv-jas2] Paul likewise speaks of "faith which worketh by love".[^kjv-gal5-6]

# Reformed view

Reformed teaching often sums up saving faith as knowledge, assent and trust. Calvin insists on knowledge: "there is no such thing as faith without knowledge".[^calvin-titus1] Faith rests on God's own word, so it "does not lean upon the authority of men; and as it leans upon God, it knows no wavering."[^calvin-2tim1] Yet assent alone is not enough. Henry: "There is not only to be assent in faith, but consent; not only an assent to the truth of the word, but a consent to take Christ." True believing is "a work of the whole heart".[^henry-jas2]

Faith is God's gift: "by grace are ye saved through faith; and that not of yourselves: it is the gift of God".[^kjv-eph2-8] To believers "it is given in the behalf of Christ ... to believe on him".[^kjv-phil1-29]

Faith saves by receiving Christ, not by any worth of its own (see [justification](/doctrines/justification.md) and [grace](/themes/grace.md)). Calvin reads Habakkuk this way: "to live by faith means to abandon voluntarily all the defenses which so often fail us", for faith "sets us naked and helpless before God".[^calvin-hab2]

Faith and works belong together, as James and Paul both show. Henry writes: "Faith is the root, good works are the fruits; and we must see to it that we have both."[^henry-jas2]

**Where other traditions differ.** These are summaries for orientation, not drawn from the corpus. **Roman Catholic** teaching, as defined at the Council of Trent, calls faith the beginning of justification but holds that it justifies only when formed by charity, and rejects justifying faith as trust alone. **Arminian and Wesleyan** theology holds that grace given to all enables a free response of faith that can be refused; Reformed theology holds that God's gift of faith is effectual in the elect. **Eastern Orthodox** theology places faith within a lifelong path of union with God in the Church and speaks less in legal terms.
