# Overview

A covenant is a solemn bond in which God commits himself to a people and claims them as his own. God initiates and guarantees it, and it frames the whole story of redemption. Its recurring formula is God's promise "to be a God unto thee, and to thy seed after thee".[^kjv-gen17]

# Development through Scripture

After the flood God pledged to Noah and all creatures that "neither shall all flesh be cut off any more by the waters of a flood".[^kjv-gen9-11] With [Abraham](/people/abraham.md) the covenant became "an everlasting covenant", with circumcision as "a token of the covenant".[^kjv-gen17] At Sinai, through Moses, Israel was bound to God with sacrifice: "Behold the blood of the covenant, which the LORD hath made with you".[^kjv-exod24-8] To David God promised that "thy throne shall be established for ever".[^kjv-2sam7-16]

Israel broke the Sinai covenant, and Jeremiah announced "a new covenant" in which God would "write it in their hearts" and "remember their sin no more".[^kjv-jer31-31] Jesus identified the cup of the Last Supper as "the new testament in my blood".[^kjv-luke22-20] Paul insists that the law given at Sinai "cannot disannul" the earlier promise confirmed "of God in Christ".[^kjv-gal3-17]

# Reformed view

Reformed (federal) theology reads Scripture through two covenants. Before the fall Adam stood under a covenant of works. Matthew Henry writes that after the fall it was "in vain for him and his to expect righteousness, life, and happiness, by the covenant of works", and that God pointed Adam instead to "the promised Seed".[^henry-gen3] All later covenants are administrations of one covenant of grace. Henry calls it "from everlasting in the counsels of it, and to everlasting in the consequences of it", and says even Abraham's blessings "were for Christ's sake".[^henry-gen17]

On this view Sinai was not a covenant of works for Israel. Henry warns against supposing "that the nation of the Jews were under the covenant of works"; it was a national covenant and "a type of the new covenant".[^henry-exod19] Calvin likewise insists that Israel, though a figure of the church, "was itself the true church", and that its promises "anticipated the gospel, so as in fact to include it".[^calvin-1cor10] The new covenant differs in fulness and power, not in substance: "God once wrote his laws to his people, now he will write his laws in them".[^henry-heb8] Reformed churches build on this continuity when they baptize the children of believers (see [sacraments](/doctrines/sacraments.md)); Henry notes that "still the encouraging promises are to us and our children".[^henry-acts2]

**Where other traditions differ.** These are summaries for orientation, not drawn from the corpus. **Dispensationalism** distinguishes more sharply between Israel and the church and between successive dispensations, and expects promises to national Israel to be fulfilled literally. **Baptists**, including Reformed Baptists, see the new covenant as made only with the regenerate and so baptize believers only. **Lutheran** theology uses covenant language less as an organising principle.
