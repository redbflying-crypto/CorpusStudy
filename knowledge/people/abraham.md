# Summary

Abraham (first called Abram) is the patriarch whom God called to leave his country, promising "in thee shall all families of the earth be blessed".[^kjv-gen12-1] When he believed God's promise, God "counted it to him for righteousness".[^kjv-gen15-5] The New Testament calls believers of every nation his children: "they which be of faith are blessed with faithful Abraham."[^kjv-gal3-7]

# Life

Abram's father Terah took the family from Ur of the Chaldees toward Canaan, and they settled in Haran.[^kjv-gen11-31] There the LORD called him, and he "departed, as the LORD had spoken unto him", at seventy-five.[^kjv-gen12-1] Childless, he was shown the stars and told "So shall thy seed be."[^kjv-gen15-5] God made a covenant with him and renamed him Abraham, "a father of many nations".[^kjv-gen17-4] In their old age Sarah bore him Isaac.[^kjv-gen21-1] God then tested him with the command to offer Isaac "for a burnt offering" on Moriah.[^kjv-gen22-2] At the last moment a ram was provided "in the stead of his son", and Abraham named the place Jehovah-jireh.[^kjv-gen22-13] He died at a hundred and seventy-five, and Isaac and Ishmael buried him at Machpelah.[^kjv-gen25-7] Genesis does not hide his failures, such as passing off Sarah as his sister in Egypt: "Say, I pray thee, thou art my sister".[^kjv-gen12-11]

# Significance

Abraham stands at the head of God's covenant people. Henry writes that God chose him "that he might reserve a people for himself ... till the coming of Christ", and that the promise of blessing to all families points to Christ, "the great blessing of the world".[^henry-gen12] Paul reads "thy seed" as "one ... which is Christ".[^kjv-gal3-16] In Reformed reading the covenant with Abraham is the covenant of grace. Henry calls it so, and says circumcision sealed "of heaven, through Christ, to the whole church of God".[^henry-gen17] Paul calls circumcision "a seal of the righteousness of the faith", making Abraham "the father of all them that believe".[^kjv-rom4-11]

Abraham is Scripture's chief example of [justification](/doctrines/justification.md) by faith. Calvin says Abraham "acquired (adeptus sit) righteousness before God, and that by imputation", and stresses that this came "many years after he had been called by God", so that "the saints are justified by grace until they die".[^calvin-gen15] Henry adds that "Abraham's faith did not justify him by its own merit or value, but as giving him a part in Christ."[^henry-rom4]

His faith was also obedience. He "went out, not knowing whither he went", looking "for a city which hath foundations, whose builder and maker is God".[^kjv-heb11-8] Calvin calls the offering of Isaac "an example of singular courage", because Isaac was "one who included Christ".[^calvin-heb11] Henry sees the ram as a figure: "Christ was sacrificed in our stead, as this ram instead of Isaac".[^henry-gen22]
