# Summary

Simon Peter was a Galilean fisherman whom Jesus called to follow him and made "fishers of men".[^kjv-matt4-18] Jesus named him Cephas, "A stone".[^kjv-john1-42] He confessed Jesus as "the Christ, the Son of the living God".[^kjv-matt16-16] After denying Jesus and being restored, he preached at Pentecost and became a leader of the early church.[^kjv-john21-15][^kjv-acts2-38] He later called himself "also an elder, and a witness of the sufferings of Christ".[^kjv-1pet5-1]

# Life

Peter walked on the water toward Jesus, but "beginning to sink" he cried "Lord, save me".[^kjv-matt14-28] At Caesarea Philippi he made his confession. Jesus answered, "thou art Peter, and upon this rock I will build my church", and promised him "the keys of the kingdom of heaven".[^kjv-matt16-16] When he then rebuked Jesus for speaking of the cross, he was told "Get thee behind me, Satan".[^kjv-matt16-22] On the night of the arrest he denied knowing Jesus, "and immediately the cock crew", and he "wept bitterly."[^kjv-matt26-74] After the resurrection Jesus asked him three times "lovest thou me?" and charged him "Feed my sheep", foretelling the death by which he would "glorify God".[^kjv-john21-15] At Pentecost he called the crowd to "Repent, and be baptized", and about three thousand were added.[^kjv-acts2-38] At Cornelius's house he learned "that God is no respecter of persons".[^kjv-acts10-34] At Antioch Paul "withstood him to the face, because he was to be blamed."[^kjv-gal2-11]

# Significance

Reformed teaching reads Matthew 16:18 as founding the church on Christ, confessed by Peter, not on Peter's person or office. Henry writes: "Nothing can be more wrong than to suppose that Christ meant the person of Peter was the rock. Without doubt Christ himself is the Rock". He adds that "Peter's confession is this rock as to doctrine", and that the keys were given to Peter speaking "in the name of his brethren".[^henry-matt16] Peter himself calls Christ "a living stone" and "a chief corner stone", and believers "lively stones".[^kjv-1pet2-4] On the power to forgive, Calvin says Christ "alone through his apostles remits sins", by their preaching of the gospel.[^calvin-john20] See [the kingdom of God](/themes/kingdom-of-god.md).

Peter's fall and restoration are a lesson in grace. Calvin calls his fall "a clear image of our own weakness", and his repentance "an unforgettable example of the goodness and mercy of God".[^calvin-matt26] Henry adds that "Peter, who wept so bitterly for denying Christ, never denied him again" (see [perseverance](/doctrines/perseverance.md)).[^henry-matt26]

These are summaries for orientation, not drawn from the corpus:

- **Roman Catholic** teaching holds that Christ made Peter himself the rock and the visible head of the apostles, and that this primacy passes to the bishops of Rome as his successors (defined at the First Vatican Council).
- **Eastern Orthodox** churches honour Peter as first among the apostles but deny that Rome holds a universal jurisdiction over the church.
- Some Protestant scholars read *this rock* as Peter himself, as the first confessor and foundational apostle. They still deny any succession of that role to a single bishop.
