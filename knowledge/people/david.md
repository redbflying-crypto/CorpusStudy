# Summary

David, the son of Jesse, was the shepherd whom God chose as king of Israel, "a man after mine own heart".[^kjv-acts13-22] He is remembered as "the sweet psalmist of Israel".[^kjv-2sam23-1] God promised him a house and a throne "established for ever",[^kjv-2sam7-12] and the New Testament proclaims Jesus as the heir of that promise.[^kjv-luke1-32]

# Life

Samuel anointed David among his brothers, "and the Spirit of the LORD came upon David from that day forward."[^kjv-1sam16-12] He faced Goliath "in the name of the LORD of hosts".[^kjv-1sam17-45] Hunted by Saul, he refused to harm "the LORD'S anointed".[^kjv-1sam24-6] He was anointed king over all Israel at thirty and reigned forty years.[^kjv-2sam5-3] When he wished to build a house for God, God promised instead to build David's house: his seed "shall build an house for my name, and I will stablish the throne of his kingdom for ever."[^kjv-2sam7-12] David then took Bathsheba, the wife of Uriah, and arranged Uriah's death in battle.[^kjv-2sam11-4] The prophet Nathan told him "Thou art the man"; David confessed "I have sinned against the LORD", and was told "The LORD also hath put away thy sin".[^kjv-2sam12-7] He died and "was buried in the city of David."[^kjv-1kgs2-10]

# Significance

In Reformed reading David is a type of Christ. Henry notes that his name "signifies Beloved; he was a type of God's beloved Son".[^henry-1sam16] The covenant promise of 2 Samuel 7 relates to Solomon, but Henry holds that the kingdom "for ever" "can be applied to no other than to Christ and his kingdom: David's house and kingdom long since came to an end."[^henry-2sam7] Peter preached that David, "being a prophet", knew God would raise up Christ "to sit on his throne".[^kjv-acts2-29] Calvin, on the promised "righteous Branch" of David, says the prophets "set Christ before men's eyes when they wish to inspire good hope in the distressed".[^calvin-jer23] See [kingdom of God](/themes/kingdom-of-god.md) and [covenant](/themes/covenant.md).

David is also a pattern of a believer who falls and is restored. Henry observes that "David says not a word to excuse himself or make light of his sin", and that even where pardon is given, "the Lord will visit the transgression of his people with the rod".[^henry-2sam12] In Psalm 51 David "flees to God's infinite mercy, and depends upon that alone for pardon and peace", and "confesses his original corruption".[^henry-ps51]

His psalms have shaped the church's prayer and [worship](/themes/worship.md). Calvin, calling him "the greatest of the psalmists", wrote that his own trials gave him "an intimate understanding of the mind of those who wrote them", and that "David showed me the way with his own footsteps".[^calvin-psalms-preface]
