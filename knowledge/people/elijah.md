# Summary

Elijah the Tishbite was a prophet in the northern kingdom of Israel in the reign of Ahab, who had married Jezebel and "served Baal, and worshipped him."[^kjv-1kgs16-30] On Mount Carmel he called the people to choose: "if the LORD be God, follow him: but if Baal, then follow him."[^kjv-1kgs18-21] He was taken up "by a whirlwind into heaven",[^kjv-2kgs2-11] and Malachi promised that God would send "Elijah the prophet before the coming of the great and dreadful day of the LORD".[^kjv-mal4-5]

# Life

Elijah announced to Ahab that there would be no dew or rain "but according to my word", and God fed him by ravens at the brook.[^kjv-1kgs17-1] At Zarephath the LORD heard his prayer and the widow's son "revived."[^kjv-1kgs17-9] On Carmel he challenged the prophets of Baal: "the God that answereth by fire, let him be God."[^kjv-1kgs18-21] Fire fell on his sacrifice, and the people cried "The LORD, he is the God".[^kjv-1kgs18-38] Then, fleeing Jezebel, he asked to die: "It is enough; now, O LORD, take away my life".[^kjv-1kgs19-1] At Horeb he complained "I, even I only, am left", heard "a still small voice", and was told God had left "seven thousand in Israel" who had not bowed to Baal.[^kjv-1kgs19-10] He called Elisha by casting his mantle on him,[^kjv-1kgs19-19] and was later parted from him by "a chariot of fire, and horses of fire".[^kjv-2kgs2-11] He appeared with Moses, "talking with" Jesus, at the transfiguration.[^kjv-matt17-3]

# Significance

Elijah stands for undivided loyalty to the LORD. Henry applies Carmel to every age: "It is dangerous to halt between the service of God and the service of sin". He also warns that most of the people "were convinced only, not converted."[^henry-1kgs18]

His despair at Horeb shows God keeping a remnant by grace. Henry writes that "God's faithful ones are often his hidden ones",[^henry-1kgs19] and Paul uses this episode to show that "there is a remnant according to the election of grace" (see [election](/doctrines/election.md)).[^kjv-rom11-2] James holds him up as a model of prayer, "a man subject to like passions as we are".[^kjv-jas5-17] Henry draws the lesson: "In prayer we must not look to the merit of man, but to the grace of God."[^henry-jas5]

His ascension points forward. Henry says it gave "a glimpse of the eternal life brought to light by the gospel" and "was also a figure of Christ's ascension".[^henry-2kgs2] Malachi's promised Elijah came in [John the Baptist](/people/john-the-baptist.md), who went "in the spirit and power of Elias".[^kjv-luke1-17] Jesus said "Elias is come already, and they knew him not", and the disciples understood "that he spake unto them of John the Baptist."[^kjv-matt17-11] Henry likewise writes that "John the Baptist preached repentance and reformation, as Elijah had done."[^henry-mal4]
