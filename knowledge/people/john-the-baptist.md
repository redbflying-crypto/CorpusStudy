# Summary

John the Baptist was the prophet "sent from God" to "bear witness of the Light, that all men through him might believe."[^kjv-john1-6] He preached "Repent ye: for the kingdom of heaven is at hand", baptized in the Jordan, and pointed to Jesus as "the Lamb of God, which taketh away the sin of the world."[^kjv-matt3-1][^kjv-john1-29]

# Life

John was born to the priest Zacharias and his wife Elisabeth, who were "well stricken in years". The angel said he would be "filled with the Holy Ghost, even from his mother's womb" and would go before the Lord "in the spirit and power of Elias".[^kjv-luke1-13] He preached in the wilderness of Judaea, dressed in camel's hair and living on "locusts and wild honey", and people were baptized by him "confessing their sins."[^kjv-matt3-1] When Jesus came to be baptized, John protested, "I have need to be baptized of thee", but Jesus answered, "thus it becometh us to fulfil all righteousness."[^kjv-matt3-13] He denied being the Christ and called himself "the voice of one crying in the wilderness".[^kjv-john1-20] As Jesus' ministry grew he said, "He must increase, but I must decrease."[^kjv-john3-30] From prison he sent to ask, "Art thou he that should come, or do we look for another?"[^kjv-matt11-2] Herod imprisoned him for rebuking his marriage to Herodias, and later had him beheaded.[^kjv-matt14-3]

# Significance

John stands at the turn from the Old Testament to the New. Henry notes that "After Malachi there was no prophet until John the Baptist came."[^henry-matt3-1] Jesus said that none born of women was greater, yet "he that is least in the kingdom of heaven is greater than he", and called him the promised "Elias, which was for to come".[^kjv-matt11-11] John himself denied being Elijah; Henry reconciles the two: "He came in the spirit and power of Elias, but he was not the person of Elias."[^henry-john1-19] See [Elijah](/people/elijah.md).

His message joined law and gospel. Calvin notes that John first "dealt with the grace of Christ" and then spoke of judgment, presenting "Christ as a dreadful judge toward unbelievers", and adds: "We also must present our doctrine in this order".[^calvin-matt3] Henry stresses that his call to repentance pointed away from himself: "he told his followers that they were to look for the pardon of their sins to Jesus only, and to his death."[^henry-john1-29] His witness to "the Lamb of God" ties Christ to the Passover and the daily sacrifices; Henry calls it "Christ slain as a sacrifice to redeem us to God by his blood" (see [atonement](/doctrines/atonement.md)).[^henry-john1-29]

Henry also sees in him a pattern for ministers: "John was a self-denying man, dead to all the pomps of the world".[^henry-matt11-7] When people "pretend to favour the gospel, yet live in evil", Henry says, we "must deliver our consciences as John did."[^henry-matt14]
