# Summary

Adam is the first man. God formed him "of the dust of the ground", and he "became a living soul".[^kjv-gen2-7] He was made in the image of God and given dominion over the earth.[^kjv-gen1-26] By his disobedience sin and death entered the world, and Paul calls him "the figure of him that was to come".[^kjv-rom5-14]

# Life

God put Adam in the garden of Eden "to dress it and to keep it". He might eat freely of every tree but one, the tree of the knowledge of good and evil, with the warning "thou shalt surely die".[^kjv-gen2-15] The woman, deceived by the serpent, ate of that tree "and gave also unto her husband with her; and he did eat."[^kjv-gen3-6] God cursed the ground for his sake and sentenced him to toil "till thou return unto the ground".[^kjv-gen3-17] He and Eve were sent out of the garden (Genesis 3:22-24). Adam lived nine hundred and thirty years, "and he died".[^kjv-gen5-5]

# Significance

Reformed theology reads Adam as the federal (covenant) head of the human race: he stood on trial for all who would descend from him. Matthew Henry writes that "everlasting life made sure to him upon his obedience", and that "the whole human race were concerned in the trial and fall of our first parents".[^henry-gen2-16] Adam's sin was "disobedience to a plain, easy, and express command", and "he drew all his posterity into sin and ruin".[^henry-gen3-6]

Paul states the link: "by one man sin entered into the world, and death by sin".[^kjv-rom5-12] Henry comments: "Adam sinning, his nature became guilty and corrupted, and so came to his children. Thus in him all have sinned."[^henry-rom5-12] Adam is a type of Christ by contrast: "as by one man's disobedience many were made sinners, so by the obedience of one shall many be made righteous."[^kjv-rom5-19] Henry adds that Christ's gift "did not place them anew in a state of trial, but fixed them in a state of justification, as Adam would have been placed, had he stood."[^henry-rom5-15] So "as in Adam all die, even so in Christ shall all be made alive", and Christ is "the last Adam".[^kjv-1cor15-22][^kjv-1cor15-45]

The way back to life was not through Adam. Henry says it was "in vain for him and his to expect righteousness, life, and happiness, by the covenant of works", and that God meant to quicken Adam "to look for life and happiness in the promised Seed".[^henry-gen3-22] See [the fall](/events/fall.md), [sin](/themes/sin.md) and [covenant](/themes/covenant.md).

These are summaries for orientation, not drawn from the corpus:

- **Roman Catholic** teaching affirms original sin from Adam but does not frame it as a covenant of works; it speaks of the loss of original holiness and justice.
- **Eastern Orthodox** theology usually speaks of *ancestral sin*: Adam's descendants inherit mortality and corruption rather than his guilt.
- Some Christians, including some Protestants, read Genesis 2-3 as figurative or treat Adam as a representative rather than the sole ancestor of humanity. The Reformed confessions affirm a historical Adam.
