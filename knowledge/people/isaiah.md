# Summary

Isaiah, "the son of Amoz", prophesied concerning Judah and Jerusalem "in the days of Uzziah, Jotham, Ahaz, and Hezekiah, kings of Judah."[^kjv-isa1-1] Matthew Henry notes that he "has been well called the evangelical prophet" because of his many prophecies of the Messiah.[^henry-isa-intro] This page is about the man and his ministry; for the book that bears his name, see [Isaiah](/books/isaiah.md).

# Life

**His call.** "In the year that king Uzziah died" Isaiah saw "the Lord sitting upon a throne, high and lifted up". He cried, "Woe is me! for I am undone; because I am a man of unclean lips". A seraph touched his mouth with a live coal from the altar: "thine iniquity is taken away, and thy sin purged". When the Lord asked, "Whom shall I send", he answered, "Here am I; send me."[^kjv-isa6-1] He was sent to a people who would "Hear ... indeed, but understand not".[^kjv-isa6-9]

**Under Ahaz.** When Syria and Israel threatened Jerusalem, Isaiah went with his son Shear-jashub to meet King Ahaz. His message was "Take heed, and be quiet; fear not", with a warning: "If ye will not believe, surely ye shall not be established."[^kjv-isa7-3] Isaiah said, "I and the children whom the LORD hath given me are for signs and for wonders in Israel".[^kjv-isa8-18]

**Under Hezekiah.** When an Assyrian army came against Ashdod, Isaiah "walked naked and barefoot three years for a sign and wonder upon Egypt and upon Ethiopia".[^kjv-isa20-2] When Sennacherib of Assyria threatened Jerusalem, Hezekiah sent to "Isaiah the prophet the son of Amoz", who answered, "Be not afraid of the words that thou hast heard".[^kjv-isa37-2] Isaiah foretold that the king of Assyria "shall not come into this city", and "the angel of the LORD went forth, and smote in the camp of the Assyrians".[^kjv-isa37-33] When Hezekiah was "sick unto death", Isaiah told him, "Set thine house in order", then brought God's answer: "I have heard thy prayer, I have seen thy tears".[^kjv-isa38-1] After Hezekiah showed his treasures to envoys from Babylon, Isaiah foretold that all of it "shall be carried to Babylon".[^kjv-isa39-5]

Scripture does not record his death; a Jewish and early Christian tradition says he was sawn in two under Manasseh (compare Hebrews 11:37). (Orientation, not drawn from the corpus.)

# Significance

**A sinner made a messenger.** In Reformed reading, Isaiah's call shows that God's holiness humbles before it sends. Calvin writes, "The whole carnal man must be reduced to nothingness that he may be renewed by God", and "Humility arises from and consists in this: that man claim nothing more for himself and depend wholly on God."[^calvin-isa6] Henry: "We are undone if there is not a Mediator between us and this holy God." He takes the live coal to "denote the assurance given to the prophet, of pardon, and acceptance in his work, through the atonement of Christ."[^henry-isa6] See [holiness](/themes/holiness.md).

**Faith rather than fear.** Henry sums up Isaiah's counsel to Ahaz: "Faith is absolutely necessary to quiet and compose the mind in trials."[^henry-isa7] See [faith](/themes/faith.md).

**Comforter and reprover.** Hezekiah asked Isaiah to "lift up thy prayer for the remnant that is left".[^kjv-isa37-2] Henry comments: "Those who speak from God to us, we should in a particular manner desire to speak to God for us."[^henry-2kgs19] He was Hezekiah's comforter and then his reprover, and Henry adds, "Ministers must be both, as there is occasion."[^henry-2kgs20]

**A witness to Christ.** John says, "These things said Esaias, when he saw his glory, and spake of him."[^kjv-john12-41] See [Jesus Christ](/people/jesus.md). Most critical scholars assign the book's later chapters to prophets after Isaiah; the Reformed tradition has generally held to his authorship (see the book concept). (Orientation, not drawn from the corpus.)
