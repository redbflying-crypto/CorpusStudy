# Summary

Moses is the prophet through whom God brought Israel out of Egypt and gave the law at Sinai. Scripture says "there arose not a prophet since in Israel like unto Moses, whom the LORD knew face to face".[^kjv-deut34-10] The New Testament sets him beside Christ: "the law was given by Moses, but grace and truth came by Jesus Christ."[^kjv-john1-17]

# Life

Born to Hebrew parents under Pharaoh's oppression, he was raised as the son of Pharaoh's daughter, who named him because "I drew him out of the water."[^kjv-exod2-1] As a grown man he killed an Egyptian who was beating a Hebrew and fled to Midian.[^kjv-exod2-11] There God appeared to him in a bush that "burned with fire, and ... was not consumed",[^kjv-exod3-2] and revealed his name: "I AM THAT I AM".[^kjv-exod3-14] Moses returned, confronted Pharaoh, and led the people out; at the Red Sea he stretched out his hand and "the waters were divided."[^kjv-exod14-21] At Sinai God gave him "tables of stone, and a law, and commandments",[^kjv-exod24-12] and spoke with him "face to face, as a man speaketh unto his friend."[^kjv-exod33-11] After he struck the rock at Meribah, God told him "ye shall not bring this congregation into the land".[^kjv-num20-12] He died in Moab at a hundred and twenty.[^kjv-deut34-5]

# Significance

Moses mediated the Sinai covenant. In the Reformed reading of [law and gospel](/themes/law-and-gospel.md), the law cannot itself give life. Henry writes that Moses dying on the border "signifies that the law made nothing perfect"; the entry into rest "was reserved for Joshua, our Lord Jesus, of whom Joshua was a type".[^henry-deut34] Calvin says that "without Christ the law is nothing but a shadow", yet "Christ himself is the soul of the law and makes it alive".[^calvin-john1]

Moses is also a type of Christ. He promised "a Prophet ... like unto me; unto him ye shall hearken".[^kjv-deut18-15] Henry reads this of Christ, who "should be like unto Moses, only above him".[^henry-deut18] Henry traces the likeness: Moses was Israel's deliverer, "lawgiver" and "judge", as Christ came "to rescue us out of the slavery of the devil".[^henry-deut34] Hebrews draws the line: Moses was faithful "as a servant", "but Christ as a son over his own house".[^kjv-heb3-5]

His faith is an example. He chose "rather to suffer affliction with the people of God", esteeming "the reproach of Christ greater riches than the treasures in Egypt".[^kjv-heb11-24] Calvin says he endured as if he "saw God alone", and draws the lesson "that the true nature of faith is to set God always before our eyes".[^calvin-heb11] Scripture records his failings too: at Meribah, Henry notes, he and Aaron "took much of the glory of this work of wonder to themselves".[^henry-num20]
