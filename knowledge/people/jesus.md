# Summary

Jesus of Nazareth is the Christ, the eternal Son of God who "was made flesh, and dwelt among us".[^kjv-john1-14] He was named Jesus because "he shall save his people from their sins".[^kjv-matt1-18] In Reformed reading he is true God and true man in one person, and the "one mediator between God and men".[^kjv-1tim2-5] His life is traced more fully in [the life of Jesus](/events/life-of-jesus.md).

# Life

Mary "was found with child of the Holy Ghost" before she and Joseph came together.[^kjv-matt1-18] Jesus was born in Bethlehem, "the city of David", and laid "in a manger; because there was no room for them in the inn."[^kjv-luke2-4] At "about thirty years of age" he began his public work.[^kjv-luke3-23] When John baptized him, the Spirit descended "like a dove", and a voice said, "This is my beloved Son, in whom I am well pleased."[^kjv-matt3-16]

He preached in Galilee: "The time is fulfilled, and the kingdom of God is at hand: repent ye, and believe the gospel."[^kjv-mark1-14] Peter later summed up his ministry: God "anointed Jesus of Nazareth with the Holy Ghost and with power: who went about doing good, and healing all that were oppressed of the devil".[^kjv-acts10-38]

He was crucified at Calvary between two malefactors and died saying, "Father, into thy hands I commend my spirit".[^kjv-luke23] He "was buried, and ... rose again the third day according to the scriptures", and was seen by Cephas, the twelve, and "above five hundred brethren at once".[^kjv-1cor15-3] Then "a cloud received him out of their sight", with the promise that "this same Jesus ... shall so come in like manner".[^kjv-acts1-9] See [the crucifixion](/events/crucifixion.md) and [the resurrection](/events/resurrection.md).

# Significance

**True God and true man.** Thomas confessed him, "My Lord and my God."[^kjv-john20-28] Calvin says the Son of God "stooped so low as to take upon himself this same flesh which is subject to so many miseries".[^calvin-john1-14] Henry bids us "Notice the two natures of Christ; his Divine nature, and human nature", adding that "he became like us in all things except sin."[^henry-phil2] See [the incarnation](/doctrines/incarnation.md) and [the Trinity](/doctrines/trinity.md).

**Prophet, priest and king.** Reformed catechisms set out Christ's work under these three offices. (Orientation, not drawn from the corpus.) As *prophet*, he is the one Moses foretold: "A prophet shall the Lord your God raise up unto you".[^kjv-acts3-22] God "Hath in these last days spoken unto us by his Son".[^kjv-heb1-1] As *priest*, he came "to give his life a ransom for many".[^kjv-mark10-45] He is "a great high priest, that is passed into the heavens", "in all points tempted like as we are, yet without sin."[^kjv-heb4-14] Henry: "Christ executed one part of his priesthood on earth, in dying for us; the other he executes in heaven, pleading the cause, and presenting the offerings of his people."[^henry-heb4] See [atonement](/doctrines/atonement.md). As *king*, he says, "All power is given unto me in heaven and in earth."[^kjv-matt28-18] Calvin warns against knowing Christ's "bare essence" while neglecting "his Kingship which consists in his power to save us", a kingship that "extends to the ends of the world".[^calvin-john1-49]

**The one mediator.** Henry writes, "Sin had made a quarrel between us and God; Jesus Christ is the Mediator who makes peace."[^henry-1tim2] Calvin argues that when the office of Mediator "was attached to Christ, all other mediators were repudiated", so that "he alone is the true Mediator".[^calvin-heb9-15]

These are summaries for orientation, not drawn from the corpus. Roman Catholic and Eastern Orthodox churches confess the same two natures, but also invoke Mary and the saints, which they hold to be subordinate to Christ's mediation; Reformed churches reject this. Lutherans and Reformed differ over how Christ's humanity shares divine attributes, which bears on the Lord's Supper. Non-trinitarian groups deny his full deity.
