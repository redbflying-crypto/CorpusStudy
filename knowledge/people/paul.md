# Summary

Paul, first known as Saul, was a Pharisee who persecuted the church until the risen Christ met him on the road to Damascus.[^kjv-phil3-5][^kjv-acts9-3] Christ called him "a chosen vessel unto me, to bear my name before the Gentiles".[^kjv-acts9-15] He became "the apostle of the Gentiles",[^kjv-rom11-13] and his letters, Romans above all, are central to Reformed teaching on grace and [justification](/doctrines/justification.md).

# Life

Saul was a Jew "born in Tarsus" and "brought up ... at the feet of Gamaliel".[^kjv-acts22-3] He was "of the tribe of Benjamin, an Hebrew of the Hebrews; as touching the law, a Pharisee".[^kjv-phil3-5] He "made havock of the church", dragging believers to prison.[^kjv-acts8-3] Near Damascus a light from heaven struck him down, and he heard "Saul, Saul, why persecutest thou me?"[^kjv-acts9-3] At Antioch the Holy Spirit said, "Separate me Barnabas and Saul for the work whereunto I have called them."[^kjv-acts13-2] His journeys planted churches across the Roman world at great cost: "Thrice was I beaten with rods, once was I stoned, thrice I suffered shipwreck".[^kjv-2cor11-24] Acts ends with him a prisoner in Rome, "Preaching the kingdom of God ... with all confidence, no man forbidding him."[^kjv-acts28-30] Facing death, he wrote: "I have fought a good fight, I have finished my course, I have kept the faith".[^kjv-2tim4-6] Scripture does not narrate his death.

# Significance

Paul's conversion shows sovereign grace. He says God "called me by his grace, to reveal his Son in me".[^kjv-gal1-15] Henry writes that "All who are savingly converted, are called by the grace of God".[^henry-gal1] He also urges: "Let us not despair of renewing grace for the conversion of the greatest sinners".[^henry-acts9] Paul counted himself "the least of the apostles", yet said "by the grace of God I am what I am".[^kjv-1cor15-9] Henry notes that "He ascribes to Divine grace all that was valuable in him."[^henry-1cor15]

His teaching sets out the gospel as "the power of God unto salvation to every one that believeth".[^kjv-rom1-16] He renounced "mine own righteousness, which is of the law" for "the righteousness which is of God by faith".[^kjv-phil3-5] Henry comments: "There is a righteousness provided for us in Jesus Christ, and it is a complete and perfect righteousness. None can have benefit by it, who trust in themselves."[^henry-phil3] Calvin prized Romans because "when anyone understands this epistle, the way is open before him to an understanding of the whole of Scripture."[^calvin-romans] See also [grace](/themes/grace.md) and [law and gospel](/themes/law-and-gospel.md).

Paul is also a model of suffering for the gospel. Calvin reads "For me to live is Christ, and to die is gain" as meaning that "having Christ, he is the gainer either way".[^calvin-phil1] On the beating of Paul and Silas at Philippi, Calvin says the Lord "did not let them suffer shame without turning it into a greater glory".[^calvin-acts16]
