# Overview

Revelation calls itself "The Revelation of Jesus Christ, which God gave unto him, to shew unto his servants things which must shortly come to pass".[^kjv-rev1-1] Matthew Henry comments that "No part of Scripture more fully states the gospel, and warns against the evil of sin."[^henry-rev1] Its aim, he says, is to "comfort the afflicted and tempted Christian, and, we may especially add, to strengthen the martyr of Christ".[^henry-intro]

**How the visions are read.** Christians agree on the book's centre, Christ's victory and return, but differ on how its visions relate to history. These are summaries for orientation, not drawn from the corpus:

- **Preterist:** most visions were fulfilled in the first century, in the fall of Jerusalem or the judgment of pagan Rome.
- **Historicist:** the visions trace church history from John's day to the end; the usual Protestant reading from the Reformation into the nineteenth century.
- **Futurist:** most of chapters 4-22 concerns events just before Christ's return.
- **Idealist:** the visions portray the conflict of Christ and Satan throughout the church age, ending in final victory.

Many Reformed readers today join an idealist reading to a future return and judgment. Henry at times reads historically: "Pagan idolatry, which was the worship of devils, was cast out of the empire by the spreading of Christianity."[^henry-rev12]

# Setting

The writer is "John", a "companion in tribulation", exiled "in the isle that is called Patmos, for the word of God, and for the testimony of Jesus Christ". He writes to seven churches in Asia, from Ephesus to Laodicea.[^kjv-rev1-9] Tradition identifies him with the apostle John. Most date the book to about AD 95 under Domitian; some, especially preterists, argue for a date before AD 70. (Orientation, not drawn from the corpus.) John is told to write "the things which are, and the things which shall be hereafter".[^kjv-rev1-19] Henry divides the book on these lines; the letters concern the churches "as they then existed", but suit "the Christian church at all times".[^henry-intro]

# Outline

- **1:** greeting; the vision of the risen Christ.
- **2-3:** letters to the seven churches.
- **4-5:** the throne of God; the Lamb takes the sealed book.
- **6:1-8:1:** the seven seals.
- **8:2-11:19:** the seven trumpets.
- **12-14:** the woman, the dragon and the beasts.
- **15-16:** the seven vials.
- **17-19:** the fall of Babylon; the rider on the white horse.
- **20:** the thousand years and the final judgment.
- **21-22:** the new heaven and new earth.

# Themes

- **The Lamb on the throne.** The "Lion of the tribe of Juda" appears as "a Lamb as it had been slain".[^kjv-rev5-5] He is worthy because he "hast redeemed us to God by thy blood".[^kjv-rev5-9] Henry: "He is called a Lion, but he appears as a Lamb slain."[^henry-rev5] His name is "KING OF KINGS, AND LORD OF LORDS".[^kjv-rev19-16] See [Jesus Christ](/people/jesus.md).
- **Faithfulness under persecution.** "be thou faithful unto death, and I will give thee a crown of life."[^kjv-rev2-10] The saints overcame Satan "by the blood of the Lamb".[^kjv-rev12-9] Henry: "The redeemed overcame by a simple reliance on the blood of Christ, as the only ground of their hopes."[^henry-rev12]
- **The thousand years.** Satan is bound "a thousand years", and the martyrs "lived and reigned with Christ a thousand years".[^kjv-rev20-1] Henry speaks of a reign "in his spiritual and heavenly kingdom", and expects that "a thousand years will follow the destruction of the antichristian, idolatrous, persecuting powers".[^henry-rev20-4] These are summaries for orientation, not drawn from the corpus. *Amillennialism* takes the thousand years as the present age, with Christ reigning from heaven; it is the most common view in Reformed churches today. *Postmillennialism* expects a long age of gospel triumph before Christ returns; many Puritans held it, and Henry's comment leans this way. *Premillennialism* expects Christ to return before an earthly reign; some Reformed hold its historic form, while the dispensational form is found mostly outside Reformed churches.
- **New creation.** "I saw a new heaven and a new earth", where "God shall wipe away all tears from their eyes".[^kjv-rev21-1] Henry: "This new Jerusalem is the church of God in its new and perfect state, the church triumphant."[^henry-rev21] The book closes, "Even so, come, Lord Jesus."[^kjv-rev22-20] See [creation](/themes/creation.md) and [the kingdom of God](/themes/kingdom-of-god.md).
