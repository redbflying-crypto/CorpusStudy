# Overview

Acts tells how the gospel spread after Jesus' ascension. Its programme is in his words: "ye shall be witnesses unto me both in Jerusalem, and in all Judaea, and in Samaria, and unto the uttermost part of the earth."[^kjv-acts1-8] Matthew Henry says the book "unites the Gospels to the Epistles". It runs "from the ascension of our Saviour to the arrival of St. Paul at Rome, a space of about thirty years".[^henry-intro]

# Setting

Acts, addressed to Theophilus, calls itself a sequel to a "former treatise" about "all that Jesus began both to do and teach".[^kjv-acts1-1] Luke's Gospel is addressed to the same "most excellent Theophilus".[^kjv-luke1] Tradition, which Henry follows, names Luke as author: "he was present at many of the events he relates, and attended Paul to Rome".[^henry-intro] Some passages shift into the first person: "immediately we endeavoured to go into Macedonia".[^kjv-acts16-10]

Henry is candid that "the narrative does not afford a complete history of the church". He also thinks most of the speeches are "only the substance of what was actually delivered".[^henry-intro]

The book ends with Paul in Rome for "two whole years", "Preaching the kingdom of God ... no man forbidding him".[^kjv-acts28] Scholars disagree whether Acts was written soon after that or decades later. (Orientation, not drawn from the corpus.)

# Outline

A common outline follows the widening circle of 1:8.[^kjv-acts1-8]

- **1-7:** Jerusalem: Pentecost, the apostles' preaching, Stephen.
- **8-12:** Judaea and Samaria; Saul's conversion; Cornelius.
- **13-20:** Paul's missionary journeys and the Jerusalem council (ch. 15).
- **21-28:** Paul's arrest, trials and voyage to Rome.

# Themes

- **The Spirit's power for witness.** At Pentecost "they were all filled with the Holy Ghost, and began to speak with other tongues".[^kjv-acts2] For Henry, one aim of the book is to show how the Spirit's gifts and the apostles' miracles served "to confirm the truth of Christianity".[^henry-intro] Many Reformed teachers tie such gifts to the church's founding; Pentecostal and charismatic churches expect them to continue. (Orientation, not drawn from the corpus.)
- **God's sovereign plan.** Jesus was "delivered by the determinate counsel and foreknowledge of God", yet slain "by wicked hands".[^kjv-acts2-23] In Reformed reading, Calvin infers that what befell Jesus "was not only foreseen by God, but also decreed by him", while the wickedness "must not be attributed to God".[^calvin-acts2] On the early church's growth Henry writes, "Those whom God has designed for eternal salvation, shall be effectually brought to Christ".[^henry-acts2-42] See [election](/doctrines/election.md).
- **Gentiles saved by grace.** [Paul](/people/paul.md) is "a chosen vessel unto me, to bear my name before the Gentiles".[^kjv-acts9-15] At the council [Peter](/people/peter.md) says, "through the grace of the Lord Jesus Christ we shall be saved, even as they."[^kjv-acts15-11] Henry concludes there that "justification by faith, and sanctification by the Holy Ghost, cannot be separated".[^henry-acts15]
