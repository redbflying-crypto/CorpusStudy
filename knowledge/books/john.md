# Overview

The fourth Gospel states its own purpose: "these are written, that ye might believe that Jesus is the Christ, the Son of God; and that believing ye might have life through his name."[^kjv-john20-31] It opens before creation: "In the beginning was the Word, and the Word was with God, and the Word was God."[^kjv-john1-1] Matthew Henry says it was written to give "just notions of the real nature, office, and character of that Divine Teacher, who came to instruct and to redeem mankind".[^henry-intro]

# Setting

The book names its source only as "the disciple whom Jesus loved", and says of him, "This is the disciple which testifieth of these things, and wrote these things".[^kjv-john21] Church tradition identifies him as the apostle John, son of Zebedee. Henry follows that tradition. He says John lived "chiefly at Ephesus", where "it is thought he wrote his Gospel and Epistles, about A. D. 97". He adds that John, "By omitting, or only briefly mentioning, the events recorded by the other evangelists", left room for teaching the others do not record.[^henry-intro]

Modern scholars debate whether the beloved disciple was the son of Zebedee and how far others shaped the final text. Many place it late in the first century, though some argue for an earlier date. (This note is for orientation, not drawn from the corpus.)

# Outline

Readers commonly see a turn at 13:1, when Jesus knew "that his hour was come that he should depart out of this world unto the Father".[^kjv-john13-1]

- **1:1-18:** prologue: the Word made flesh.
- **1:19-12:50:** public ministry: signs, discourses and growing unbelief.
- **13-17:** the upper room: foot washing, farewell teaching and Jesus' prayer.
- **18-20:** arrest, trial, crucifixion and resurrection.
- **21:** epilogue: Peter restored; the beloved disciple's witness.

# Themes

- **The Son is God made flesh.** Henry writes, "What the evangelist says of Christ proves that he is God."[^henry-john1] "The Word was made flesh, and dwelt among us".[^kjv-john1-14] Calvin says the Son "for our sakes, left the height of his heavenly glory and humbled himself to a state at once low and abject".[^calvin-john1-14] Jesus' "I am" sayings point the same way: "I am the bread of life".[^kjv-john6-35]
- **Believing and life.** Whoever believes "should not perish, but have everlasting life".[^kjv-john3-16]
- **God's love and election.** In Reformed reading, Calvin takes John 3:16 to "establish the love of God as the ground of our salvation". God "invites all men without exception to faith in Christ", yet "God opens the eyes of the elect alone".[^calvin-john3-16] See [election](/doctrines/election.md). Arminian readers take the verse instead as showing grace that every hearer may accept or resist. (This note is for orientation, not drawn from the corpus.)
