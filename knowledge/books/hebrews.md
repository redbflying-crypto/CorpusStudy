# Overview

Hebrews opens: "God, who at sundry times and in divers manners spake in time past unto the fathers by the prophets, Hath in these last days spoken unto us by his Son".[^kjv-heb1-1] It shows the Son greater than angels, Moses and the Levitical priests. Matthew Henry says it "shows Christ as the end, foundation, body, and truth of the figures of the law".[^henry-intro]

# Setting

The book names no author. It calls itself "the word of exhortation", mentions "our brother Timothy", and passes on greetings from "They of Italy".[^kjv-heb13-22] The writer received the gospel as "confirmed unto us by them that heard him", which suggests he was not himself an eyewitness.[^kjv-heb2-3]

Henry assumes Paul wrote it. He says that from prison in Rome "the apostle wrote the epistles to the Ephesians, Philippians, Colossians, and Hebrews".[^henry-acts28] Calvin's comment on 11:1 compares what "the apostle" teaches with "the same thing that Paul does in Rom. 8:24". There he does not name the author of Hebrews.[^calvin-heb11] Outside this corpus, Calvin's commentary on Hebrews doubted that Paul was the author. Barnabas, Apollos and others have been proposed; the question remains open. Many date the letter before AD 70, but this is debated. (Orientation, not drawn from the corpus.)

Henry describes the readers as converted Jews under pressure: the "unconverted Jews used many arguments to draw their converted brethren from the Christian faith".[^henry-intro]

# Outline

- **1-2:** the Son above the angels; a first warning (2:1-4).
- **3-4:** Christ above Moses; the promised rest.
- **5-7:** a priest after the order of Melchizedek; warning (5:11-6:12).
- **8:1-10:18:** the new covenant and the one sacrifice.
- **10:19-12:29:** draw near and endure; the faithful of chapter 11.
- **13:** closing exhortations and greetings.

# Themes

- **A better priest and sacrifice.** "we have a great high priest, that is passed into the heavens", one "in all points tempted like as we are, yet without sin."[^kjv-heb4-14] "by one offering he hath perfected for ever them that are sanctified."[^kjv-heb10] See [atonement](/doctrines/atonement.md).
- **A better covenant.** Christ is "the mediator of a better covenant, which was established upon better promises."[^kjv-heb8-6] Henry: "God once wrote his laws to his people, now he will write his laws in them".[^henry-heb8] See [covenant](/themes/covenant.md).
- **Faith that endures.** "Now faith is the substance of things hoped for, the evidence of things not seen."[^kjv-heb11-1] Calvin says the writer is not giving a full definition; faith here "is always bound up with patience".[^calvin-heb11] Believers run "Looking unto Jesus the author and finisher of our faith".[^kjv-heb12-1]
- **Warnings and perseverance.** It is "impossible for those who were once enlightened ... If they shall fall away, to renew them again unto repentance".[^kjv-heb6-4] In Reformed reading, Henry denies the passage proves "that any one who is made a new creature in Christ, ever becomes a final apostate from him". He takes it as "an open and avowed renouncing of Christ".[^henry-heb6] Arminian and Wesleyan readers take these warnings to show that true believers can fall away. (Orientation, not drawn from the corpus.) See [perseverance](/doctrines/perseverance.md).
