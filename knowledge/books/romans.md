# Overview

Romans is Paul's fullest statement of the gospel, "the power of God unto salvation to every one that believeth; to the Jew first, and also to the Greek."[^kjv-rom1-16] Matthew Henry sums up its teaching as "a sinner's acceptance with God, or justification in his sight, merely by grace, through faith in the righteousness of Christ, without distinction of nations".[^henry-intro] Calvin, dedicating his commentary in 1539, wrote that "when anyone understands this epistle, the way is open before him to an understanding of the whole of Scripture."[^calvin-grynaeus]

# Setting

The letter comes from "Paul, a servant of Jesus Christ".[^kjv-rom1-1] Its scribe greets the readers too: "I Tertius, who wrote this epistle".[^kjv-rom16-22] It is addressed "To all that be in Rome, beloved of God, called to be saints".[^kjv-rom1-7] Paul had not yet visited them. He writes as he goes "unto Jerusalem to minister unto the saints", hoping to see Rome on the way to Spain.[^kjv-rom15] Paul's authorship is almost universally accepted; a date in the mid-50s, probably from Corinth, is commonly proposed. (Orientation, not drawn from the corpus.)

Henry sees a mixed church: Paul's aim was "to answer the unbelieving, and to teach the believing Jew; to confirm the Christian and to convert the idolatrous Gentile".[^henry-intro]

# Outline

Henry notes that in 1:16-17 "the apostle opens the design of the whole epistle".[^henry-rom1-16]

- **1:1-17:** greeting and theme.
- **1:18-3:20:** Jew and Gentile alike under sin.
- **3:21-5:21:** justification by faith; Abraham; Adam and Christ.
- **6-8:** new life in Christ and the Spirit.
- **9-11:** God's purpose for Israel and the nations.
- **12:1-15:13:** the life of grateful obedience.
- **15:14-16:27:** Paul's plans and greetings.

# Themes

- **Sin.** "all have sinned, and come short of the glory of God".[^kjv-rom3-23] "by one man sin entered into the world".[^kjv-rom5-12] Henry writes, "Adam sinning, his nature became guilty and corrupted, and so came to his children."[^henry-rom5] See [the fall](/events/fall.md).
- **Justification.** Sinners are "justified freely by his grace through the redemption that is in Christ Jesus".[^kjv-rom3-23] Henry stresses that "It is not from faith to works, as if faith put us into a justified state, and then works kept us in it".[^henry-rom1-16] See [justification](/doctrines/justification.md). Roman Catholic teaching and the New Perspective on Paul read this language differently. (Orientation, not drawn from the corpus.)
- **Election.** "whom he called, them he also justified: and whom he justified, them he also glorified."[^kjv-rom8-28] In Reformed reading, Calvin holds that "all things which issue in the salvation of the saints depend upon the free election of God as their first cause".[^calvin-rom8] Arminian readers often take chapters 9-11 to concern peoples and roles more than individual salvation. (Orientation, not drawn from the corpus.) See [election](/doctrines/election.md).
- **Grateful obedience.** "present your bodies a living sacrifice".[^kjv-rom12-1] Calvin: "the Lord has redeemed us for no other purpose than that we may consecrate ourselves and all our members to him".[^calvin-rom12]
