# Overview

Exodus is the second book of the Law. Matthew Henry describes it as relating "the forming of the children of Israel into a church and a nation", and explains that its name signifies "the departure" from Egypt.[^henry-exod-intro] It opens with Israel enslaved, when the Egyptians "made their lives bitter with hard bondage",[^kjv-exod1-13] and ends with God dwelling among his people: "the glory of the LORD filled the tabernacle."[^kjv-exod40-34]

# Setting

Exodus continues the story of Genesis. Jacob's descendants have multiplied in Egypt and been enslaved, and "God remembered his covenant with Abraham, with Isaac, and with Jacob."[^kjv-exod2-24] Jewish and Christian tradition, the Reformed churches included, has received it as the second book of Moses. Scripture places the start of work on Solomon's temple "in the four hundred and eightieth year after the children of Israel were come out of the land of Egypt".[^kjv-1kgs6-1]

**Where other traditions differ.** These are summaries for orientation, not drawn from the corpus. Many who take 1 Kings 6:1 at face value place the exodus in the fifteenth century BC; others, including some conservative scholars, prefer the thirteenth century and read the number as schematic. Many critical scholars doubt Mosaic authorship, regard the book as composed later from several sources, and question how much of the narrative is historical.

# Outline

- **Chapters 1-4:** Israel's bondage, Moses' birth and his call at the burning bush.
- **Chapters 5-11:** Moses before Pharaoh and the plagues on Egypt.
- **Chapters 12-15:** the Passover, the departure and the crossing of the sea (see [the Exodus](/events/exodus.md)).
- **Chapters 16-18:** the journey to Sinai: manna, water from the rock, Jethro's counsel.
- **Chapters 19-24:** the covenant at Sinai and the Ten Commandments.
- **Chapters 25-31:** instructions for the tabernacle and priesthood.
- **Chapters 32-34:** the golden calf, Moses' intercession and the covenant renewed.
- **Chapters 35-40:** the tabernacle built and filled with God's glory.

# Themes

- **The name of God.** God reveals himself as "I AM THAT I AM".[^kjv-exod3-14] Henry takes it to mean that God is "self-existent", "eternal and unchangeable", and "faithful and true to all his promises".[^henry-exod3] Later the LORD proclaims himself "merciful and gracious, longsuffering, and abundant in goodness and truth" yet one who "will by no means clear the guilty".[^kjv-exod34-6]
- **Redemption before law.** The commandments begin: "I am the LORD thy God, which have brought thee out of the land of Egypt, out of the house of bondage."[^kjv-exod20-2] In Reformed reading the law is given to a people already redeemed. Henry warns: "We must not suppose that the nation of the Jews were under the covenant of works", for the Sinai covenant "was a type of the new covenant".[^henry-exod19] He also stresses the law's depth: "Its grand demand is love".[^henry-exod20] See [law and gospel](/themes/law-and-gospel.md).
- **A holy people.** Israel is called to be "a kingdom of priests, and an holy nation."[^kjv-exod19-4]
- **God's presence and worship.** The tabernacle is the place where God dwells among his people. Henry notes that Moses could not enter for the glory, "But what Moses could not do, our Lord Jesus has done".[^henry-exod40] See [worship](/themes/worship.md) and [Moses](/people/moses.md).

# Key passages

Exodus 3:1-15 (the burning bush and the divine name); Exodus 12:1-14 (the Passover); Exodus 14:13-31 (the crossing of the sea); Exodus 19:3-6 (a kingdom of priests); Exodus 20:1-17 (the Ten Commandments); Exodus 34:5-7 (the LORD proclaims his name); Exodus 40:34-38 (the glory fills the tabernacle).
