# Overview

Matthew opens the New Testament with "The book of the generation of Jesus Christ, the son of David, the son of Abraham."[^kjv-matt1-1] Matthew Henry notes that the genealogy "proves that our Lord Jesus is of the nation and family out of which the Messiah was to arise".[^henry-matt1] The Gospel tells of Jesus' birth, teaching, death and resurrection, and closes with the risen Lord sending his disciples to "teach all nations".[^kjv-matt28]

# Setting

The Gospel does not name its author, though it calls the apostle "Matthew the publican".[^kjv-matt10-3] Church tradition, which Henry follows, holds that he wrote it: he was "a publican, or tax-gatherer under the Romans at Capernaum", and that the book "was written primarily for the use of the Jewish nation".[^henry-intro]

Henry adds that Matthew "is generally allowed to have written his Gospel before any other of the evangelists".[^henry-intro] Many modern scholars instead think Mark came first and Matthew used it, and they debate whether the book dates from before or after AD 70. (This note is for orientation, not drawn from the corpus.)

# Outline

Many readers see five long discourses, each closing with a formula like "when Jesus had ended these sayings"[^kjv-matt7-28] (see also 11:1; 13:53; 19:1; 26:1).

- **1-4:** birth, baptism and temptation.
- **5-7:** the Sermon on the Mount.
- **8-10:** miracles; the sending of the Twelve.
- **11-13:** opposition; parables of the kingdom.
- **14-18:** Peter's confession; teaching on the church.
- **19-25:** Jerusalem; the discourse on the end of the age.
- **26-28:** passion, resurrection and the Great Commission.

# Themes

- **Fulfilment.** Events happen "that it might be fulfilled which was spoken of the Lord by the prophet".[^kjv-matt1-22] For Calvin it can be more than prediction: on Rachel weeping (2:17-18) Matthew meant "a recurrence of the affliction which the Benjamites suffered many centuries before".[^calvin-matt2]
- **The kingdom of heaven.** Jesus begins: "Repent: for the kingdom of heaven is at hand."[^kjv-matt4-17] See [the kingdom of God](/themes/kingdom-of-god.md).
- **Law and righteousness.** Jesus came "not ... to destroy, but to fulfil".[^kjv-matt5-17] In Reformed reading the law stays "the Christian's rule of duty", yet "Christ's righteousness, imputed to us by faith alone, is needed by every one that enters the kingdom".[^henry-matt5] See [law and gospel](/themes/law-and-gospel.md).
- **Christ and his church.** Peter confesses, "Thou art the Christ, the Son of the living God."[^kjv-matt16] Henry, as a Protestant, denies "that Christ meant the person of Peter was the rock". For him "Christ himself is the Rock", and "Peter's confession is this rock as to doctrine".[^henry-matt16] Roman Catholic teaching instead reads this verse as founding a lasting office on [Peter](/people/peter.md) himself (a note for orientation, not drawn from the corpus).
